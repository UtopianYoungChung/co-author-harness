# STYLE COMMITMENTS — Declared Methodological Position

**Purpose.** This file makes explicit a fact that the package's tone has elsewhere left implicit: the prose-craft, narrative, and theoretical-shape rules carried by `bacon_2009_well_crafted_sentence_guidelines.md`, `Sexton_Fiction_to_Academic_Writing_Guide.md`, `baird_2021_writing_guidelines.md`, and the Suchman-inflected register favoured throughout `MASTER_research_and_paper_guidelines.md` are **methodological commitments**, not universal hygiene. They reflect a stance the author has taken about what good academic writing looks like in this research program. They are *defensible*, but they are not *neutral*; treating them as neutral would conceal an argument and pre-empt legitimate disagreement from venues, reviewers, or co-authors who hold different positions.

**Status.** Read by the Planner at classification time, by the Generator before any prose-shaping action, and by the Reflector when assessing whether voice drift is a *violation* (against a declared commitment) or a *legitimate variation* (the commitment itself is what's contested). Cross-referenced from `.paper-package/CLAUDE.md` §3 and from MASTER §A.

These commitments are mutually reinforcing but *separable*. A piece can satisfy C-1 and C-2 while genuinely needing to violate C-3 (e.g., a methods note that is structurally a list, not an arc). **C-7 is the protective counterpart to the convergent commitments**: where C-1…C-4 and C-8 say what the prose should move *toward*, C-7 names what the prose must not *lose*. The two can be in tension — a strongly Suchman (C-1) rewrite can flatten an author whose natural voice is not first-person — and that tension is resolved by surfacing to the user (§6), not by silent default. **C-8 (analytic-construction discipline) is a convergent commitment like C-1…C-4, occupying the analytic-move layer between C-2 (clause craft) and C-4 (static theory anatomy)**: C-4 audits whether a theory *has* the right parts; C-8 audits whether those parts were *earned* by the argument. C-8 carries two protective carve-outs of its own — demonstrative anaphora (M-4) and cadential verdicts (M-5) are argumentative features the mechanical craft checks must not strip, mirroring C-7's idiolect carve-out. C-5 is the **meta-rule that operationalises** C-1…C-4: it is binding at every P-stage and every tier rung (T2 onward), and it is the only commitment that cannot be suspended via the §4 relaxation procedure — accessibility is Hard Constraint #8 of the Ph.D.-root CLAUDE.md and inherits the binding force of §9 (non-negotiable rules). The Planner names which of C-1…C-4, C-6, C-7, and C-8 apply to the current piece at classification time; the Generator and Evaluator hold the piece against *those* plus C-5 always.

## 1.0a C-6 rhetorical-analytical separation and scoped metaphor (added 2026-06-18)

C-6 governs prose where a term, image, or model does double duty as both an evocative presentation device and an analytical category. It is especially relevant to theory-shape language such as `layer`, `cube`, `above`, `below`, `control`, `weight`, `machine`, `system`, `AI`, `human`, `static`, and any project-specific metaphor used to structure an argument.

**Analytical wording discipline.** When the same concept is meant, use the same term. Do not vary terminology for color if the terms are analytically equivalent. Before drafting or revising a theory paragraph, identify the load-bearing terms and ask whether each term's intended meaning is clear, shared with the likely reader, and distinguishable from adjacent usages.

**Rhetorical device separation.** Rhetorical devices may be used to make a passage evocative, persuasive, or readable, but the Generator must not let them carry an unstated analytical commitment. The Evaluator should flag a C-6 issue when a sentence's force depends on color, flourish, or emotional emphasis while the analytical claim remains underspecified.

**Metaphor scope rule.** Every load-bearing metaphor must state what the metaphor is being used for and which aspect is load-bearing. If the prose says a construct is `above` another, it must say whether `above` means authority, control, abstraction level, implementation dependency, visibility, or something else. If the prose says a relation has `weight`, it must say whether weight is a numeric/scalar variable, a qualitative salience marker, or only a metaphor.

**Ontology follow-through.** When a metaphor supplies structure, the next analytical question is ontological: what entities, relations, boundaries, and role distinctions does the metaphor imply? For layer/cube/spatial metaphors, the Generator must test whether the relation is actually vertical, horizontal, reversible, or situation-dependent rather than assuming the metaphor's default geometry.

**Application default.** The Planner records C-6 as applicable whenever a paper, thesis chapter, memo, response letter, or section uses load-bearing theory terms, spatial/mechanical metaphors, or an ontology/modeling vocabulary. The Generator may invoke it by name ("applying C-6: scoped metaphor"). The Evaluator names it explicitly in findings (for example, `[MAJOR - C-6: scoped-metaphor violation]`) so the author can dispute the commitment or the finding.

**Interaction with C-8/M-1 (upfront glossary vs. deferred derivation).** C-6's "one word, one meaning" discipline pushes toward a front-loaded terminological glossary; C-8/M-1 (definitional deferral) pushes toward deriving a contested construct in the body rather than stipulating it up front. These pull against each other only at the surface, and the reconciliation is a rule: **an upfront glossary is compatible with M-1 when it carries definitional *keys only* (the single sense of each term) and defers all *argumentative or derived* content to the body.** A glossary entry that pre-announces the payoff of the very construct the argument exists to earn is both a weak M-1 move (it deflates the derivation) and outside what C-6 requires (C-6 wants consistent *sense*, not pre-stated *conclusions*). Surface this as a `[MINOR — C-6↔C-8/M-1 interaction]` note, not as a violation of either commitment alone. *(Added 2026-07-01 from the QE2026 reciprocity-draft live-run; full case in `analytic_construction_guidelines.md §2 M-1`.)*

## 1.0c C-7 authorial voice-fingerprint preservation (added 2026-06-30)

C-7 protects "the smell of the writer" — the author's idiolect — from being flattened by the convergent commitments (C-1…C-4), the accessibility pass (C-5), or any craft skill. It is the only commitment that is **protective rather than convergent**: it does not specify a register to write toward; it specifies features the pipeline must not strip without an independent warrant. Full substrate and grounding quotations: `voice_preservation_guidelines.md`.

**The mechanics/identity distinction.** C-7 rests on a split all four craft authorities share. Rules legitimately operate on the **mechanics layer** — clutter, error, vagueness, dead metaphor, broken parallelism, cliché, genuine comprehension defects. Rules destroy what they touch on the **identity layer** — sentence-length signature, repetition tolerance, point of view, cadence, humor, evaluative stance, characteristic metaphor. *Mechanics serve identity; they never overwrite it.* Strunk's "omit needless words," Zinsser's pruning, and Bacon's craft (C-2) all stay valid; what C-7 forbids is letting that discipline cross into the identity layer without a content defect.

**Idiolect as non-target.** The identity-layer features listed above are **not targets for "improvement"** absent an independently established content or accessibility (C-5) defect. A style edit that touches them must cite a correctness, clutter, or C-5 warrant — taste alone is insufficient. This is the style-side parallel of the Grounding Protocol's no-fabrication rule: the Grounding Protocol forbids inventing content; C-7 forbids erasing voice. **C-7 protects *disciplined* idiolect, not every recurring weakness:** a feature qualifies only if it is recurrent, not a comprehension defect, and plausibly intentional. A recurring mechanical error or an LLM tic that survived earlier rounds is still a defect and is not shielded (see `voice_preservation_guidelines.md` §2).

**Baseline-before-register scoring.** Voice must be scored against the *author's own baseline* (prior accepted prose, or the least-revised passages of the current draft) before it is scored against any borrowed register. A "voice degraded" finding must distinguish drift from *this author* (a real C-7 defect) from drift from the borrowed exemplar (often acceptable; possibly a C-1 matter the author may dispute). **Baseline confidence is tiered:** a baseline from accepted prior prose carries full force; a baseline from only the least-revised current draft is **provisional** — C-7 findings drop to [MINOR]/ADVISORY and craft flags are noted rather than suppressed until accepted prose exists or the author confirms the pattern. `SAFEGUARD_LAYER.md` Check 6 carries the idiolect-baseline step.

**Anti-patterns.** C-7 specifically prohibits: readability-score optimisation (Moran: "A sentence written solely via some slide-rule calculus of readability will never be quite good enough"); plain-style-as-neutrality (Moran: "The plain style is just that — a style"); per-subject re-registering (Zinsser: "Don't alter your voice to fit your subject"); amputating an opening's voice before the writer appears (Zinsser's first-paragraph test); and pigeonholing wording to a target register, since for Abbott the final wording "is an arbitrary — even an aesthetic — decision."

**Application default.** The Planner records C-7 as applicable to **any piece written in the author's own voice** — i.e., the default for research papers, thesis chapters, memos, and response letters. C-7 is *suspended* (not applicable) only where the author has chosen a borrowed or house voice for the piece (e.g., a venue-mandated impersonal register), recorded via the §4 relaxation procedure. The Generator may invoke it by name ("applying C-7: preserving sentence-length signature"). The Evaluator names it explicitly in findings (for example, `[MAJOR — C-7: idiolect flattened by register rewrite]`) so the author can dispute the commitment or the finding.

**Provenance.** Extracted 2026-06-30 from four craft authorities supplied by the author (Moran, *First You Write a Sentence*; Zinsser, *On Writing Well*; Strunk, *The Elements of Style*, original edition; Abbott, *Methods of Discovery* and *Digital Paper*), corroborated by Turabian ch. 11 and the Penguin "Good style" chapter. All quotations verified against the source texts. Rationale memo: `docs/analysis/2026-06-30_voice-fingerprint-analysis-and-C7-rationale.md`. *Edition caveat:* the attached *Elements of Style* is Strunk's original, not Strunk & White; White's "Approach to Style" essay is not relied on here.

## 1.0d C-8 analytic-construction discipline (added 2026-07-01)

C-8 governs the **analytic-move layer** — the unit of prose between the sentence (C-2/Bacon) and the whole-piece arc (C-3/Sexton), and between the theory's static anatomy (C-4/Baird) and its voice (C-1/C-7). It asks a question none of the other commitments ask: **was this claim *earned*, or merely asserted?** Full substrate, grounding quotations, and operational tests: `analytic_construction_guidelines.md`.

**The gap C-8 fills.** C-4 (Baird) audits whether a theory *has* the right parts — phenomenon, artefact, mechanism, outcome, contribution. It does not audit whether those parts were *derived*. A manuscript can satisfy the five-element model with every key term stipulated by fiat, every rival merely contradicted, and every claim asserted flat. That vacancy sat between C-2 and C-4 with no claimant — and, as with idiolect before C-7, an unowned feature gets mishandled by whatever adjacent check is nearest (a mechanical monotony flag reading Abbott's demonstrative anaphora as a rhythm defect). C-8 claims the vacancy.

**The seven moves (M-1…M-7).** Grounded in a single sustained exemplar, Abbott's *The System of Professions* (1988): **M-1** *definitional deferral* — derive a contested central term from the theory's questions, do not stipulate it up front ("Definitions, then, must follow from theoretical questions"); **M-2** *reconstruct-then-dissolve* — state a rival charitably, name its buried assumption, and dissolve the dispute by moving up a level rather than contradicting it head-on; **M-3** *counterexample-as-demolition* — break a rival category with a specific case it mis-sorts, not an abstract objection; **M-4** *anaphoric demonstration* — convert a general claim into a demonstrated pattern with parallel-framed instances ("Thus… Thus… Thus…"); **M-5** *cadential verdict* — resolve a long qualifying period with a short declarative that lands the point; **M-6** *calibrated confidence* — grade confidence to the evidence and mark judgment calls as decisions; **M-7** *meta-reflexivity* — examine the analytic apparatus's own assumptions inside the argument that uses it.

**Protective carve-outs (M-4, M-5).** Two moves are argumentative features that mechanical craft checks can mis-read, so C-8 shields them the way C-7 shields idiolect. Demonstrative anaphora (M-4) is *evidence*, not monotony — `sentence-level-pass` must not flag it, and a prior monotony flag is overturned by this commitment. A cadential verdict (M-5) is doing argumentative work — a "concision" or accessibility edit that deletes it or merges it back into the period must cite an independent warrant, or it is an M-5 regression. In both cases the burden of proof sits on the rule that would strip the feature.

**P-stage gating.** M-1, M-2, and M-7 are **P2-stage moves** — they presuppose a research problem sharp enough to argue; firing them against a P0/P1 phenomenon-collection piece is itself a stage error (cf. `p-stage-checker`). At P0/P1, C-8 is limited to M-3, M-5, and M-6, which are stage-neutral. This gating stops C-8 from pushing a characterization paper to over-claim.

**Application default.** The Planner records C-8 as applicable to any piece that **builds or extends a theory or a conceptual argument** — research papers making a theoretical contribution, thesis theory chapters, P2-stage manuscripts, conceptual sections of applied work, **and advisor-facing conceptual briefs or working drafts toward a proposal that stake a theoretical position** (the genre label "memo"/"brief" does not exclude them — the presence of a built conceptual argument decides). It is **not** applied by default to pure methods notes, logistics/status memos, report-only results sections, response letters, or P0/P1 phenomenon-collection pieces. The Generator may invoke a move by name ("applying C-8/M-1: deriving the construct from the §1 questions"). The Evaluator names the move in findings (for example, `[MAJOR — C-8/M-2: rival strawmanned, not dissolved]`) so the author can dispute the commitment or the finding. **The C-6↔M-1 upfront-glossary interaction is adjudicated per §1.0a** (a keys-only glossary is compatible; a glossary that pre-states a derived construct's payoff is a `[MINOR — C-6↔C-8/M-1]` note). Standalone entry points: `skills/analytic-move-audit/SKILL.md` (SK-42, all seven moves); `skills/definition-derivation-check/SKILL.md` (SK-43, M-1 alone); `skills/dissolution-move-check/SKILL.md` (SK-44, M-2 alone).

**Reflexive note.** Abbott's *substance* — a theory of how a division of expert labor allocates jurisdiction — also reads back onto the harness's own division of labor among agents and skills. That mapping (jurisdiction claimed in legal/public/workplace arenas; settlements beyond subordination; vacancies pulling in claimants) is recorded as a diagnostic lens, not a rearchitecture, in `analytic_construction_guidelines.md §6` and the rationale memo §6.

## 1.1 C-1 verb-set and parallelism rules (added v0.13.0)

C-1's "first-person navigation" commitment surfaces in practice through a canonical verb-set and a parallelism discipline. Both are operational specifications of C-1, not separate commitments.

**Canonical verb-set.** The Suchman-inflected register prefers eight first-person verbs at the points where authorial judgment is being made: `I identify` (for hidden assumptions), `I read [X] as [Y]ing` (for inferential moves whose outcome the source author may not endorse), `I press [N] questions against [Z]` (for questions-framing predications), `I find` (for evaluative verdicts), `I trace` (for genealogies and provenance moves), `I group` (for category construction), `I mark` (for stipulative gestures), `I characterize` (for paraphrases that the source author may not endorse). Direct attribution (`Author et al. argue/concede/flag`) is preferred when the author would endorse the characterization; the `I read [X] as [Y]ing` indirect pattern is preferred when the assumption is implicit/contestable and the source author may not endorse.

**Parallelism discipline.** When the same speech-act recurs at structurally parallel sites — hidden-assumption diagnoses across sibling subsections, questions-framing predications across position subsections, judgment-attribution moments across cited findings — those sites must ship in a single voice. Drift between sibling sites is a parallelism failure even if each site is individually C-1-compliant. **Practical rule:** when one sibling site is voice-corrected, the rest must be audited within the same revision round. The Reflector's voice audit (Check 6) treats sibling-site drift as a higher severity than isolated drift, on the rationale that parallelism failures compound the reader's load.

**Provenance.** Verb-set and parallelism rules surfaced from the INF3006Y late-April 2026 sessions (handoffs `2026-04-29-204048` through `2026-04-30-180606` plus the v0.13.0 voice round 2). Source memo: `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-04-30-voice-and-h-lessons.md` cluster 3.1.

## 2. Why declare them

Three reasons, in increasing order of importance:

1. **Falsifiability.** A rule that is never named cannot be challenged. Declaring the commitments lets a reviewer, co-author, or the author themselves contest a specific commitment without having to contest the whole harness.

2. **Venue alignment.** Some target venues (e.g., MISQ Theory & Review, JAIS) are friendly to C-1 and C-4. Others (e.g., NeurIPS, parts of CHI) are not. Pretending the commitments are neutral leads to wasted rounds where the harness drives the manuscript away from venue fit. With the commitments declared, the Planner can decide at classification time which to relax.

3. **Reflexive coherence.** The research program this package serves studies *how delegated AI agency reshapes professional identity in knowledge work*. A package that imposes its own aesthetic position on the author without naming the position would itself be an instance of the failure mode the program is theorising. Declaring the commitments is therefore not a courtesy; it is a coherence requirement (parallel to `DRIFT_CHECK.md` §6 and `REFLEXIVITY_CHECK.md` §6).

## 4. Relaxation procedure

Any commitment can be relaxed for a given project, paper type, or section, by:

1. The user issuing an explicit relaxation in `research_notes/directives.md` (e.g., `D-09: For the §4 methods of project X, suspend C-3 (narrative arc) — the section is intentionally a structured list per venue convention.`).
2. The Planner recording the suspension at the top of the revision plan.
3. The Evaluator and Generator treating the suspended commitment as not-applicable for the scoped portion of the piece. Other commitments remain in force.

Relaxation is a normal operation, not an exception. The commitments are positions, not laws — **with the exception of C-5**. **C-8 relaxes like C-1…C-4** (convergent, off unless the Planner records it as applicable), and additionally supports **per-move suspension**: a venue that expects flat assertion or an up-front stipulated-definitions table may suspend a single move without suspending the whole commitment (e.g., `D-NN: For project X's systems-venue submission, suspend C-8/M-1 — the venue expects a stipulated definitions table in §2`). C-8 is also **P-stage-gated by construction**: M-1/M-2/M-7 do not fire below P2 even when C-8 is applicable, so no directive is needed to keep them off a phenomenon-collection piece. **C-7 relaxes in the opposite direction from C-1…C-4:** it is on by default and is *suspended* by an explicit directive when the author chooses a borrowed or house voice for a piece (e.g., `D-NN: For project X's venue submission, suspend C-7 — adopt the venue's impersonal house register`). Suspending C-7 hands the identity layer to the borrowed register; it does not license arbitrary taste edits, which the mechanics/grounding rules still govern. C-5 (reader-accessibility) is the meta-rule that operationalises C-1…C-4; it is bound to Hard Constraint #8 of the Ph.D.-root CLAUDE.md and to the absolute binding force of that file's §9. C-5 cannot be suspended by directive, by venue convention, or by P-stage. A project that legitimately needs a denser register (e.g., a formal proofs section) may reduce the severity floor of specific C-5 Sub-checks via a project-scoped override recorded in `directives.md`, but may not disable C-5 wholesale.

## 5. What this prohibits

- **Tacit imposition.** The Generator may not apply prose patterns drawn from C-1…C-4 to a piece for which the Planner has not recorded the commitment as applicable.
- **Performative neutrality.** The Evaluator may not flag a violation of C-1…C-4, C-6, C-7, or C-8 with language that implies the rule is neutral hygiene (e.g., "the prose is bad" rather than "C-2 sentence-rhythm violation," "this metaphor is confusing" rather than "C-6 scoped-metaphor violation," "this sentence is too long / too repetitive" rather than "C-7: this is the author's baseline idiolect, not a defect," or "this argument is weak" rather than "C-8/M-2: rival contradicted, not dissolved"). The commitment must be named so the author can dispute it.
- **Cross-commitment over-claim.** A finding tied to one commitment may not be presented as evidence for the others. C-1 and C-3 are independent stances; satisfying one does not satisfy the other. C-4 (theory has the right parts) and C-8 (those parts were earned) are likewise independent — a five-element-complete theory is not thereby a well-argued one.
- **Idiolect-stripping on taste (C-7).** No agent or skill may strip an identity-layer feature — sentence-length signature, repetition tolerance, point of view, cadence, humor, evaluative stance, characteristic metaphor — on taste alone. The edit must cite an independent correctness, clutter, or C-5 accessibility warrant, or it is a C-7 violation. Voice must be scored against the author's own baseline before any borrowed register.
- **Argument-feature stripping on "concision" (C-8).** No agent or skill may flatten demonstrative anaphora (M-4) as monotony, or delete/bury a cadential verdict (M-5) as clutter, on taste or a mechanical craft flag alone. A `sentence-level-pass` flag on either feature is overturned by C-8 unless an independent evidential or C-5 warrant is cited. Nor may a contested central construct be stipulated by fiat where the theory could derive it (M-1), or a rival be strawmanned rather than reconstructed and dissolved (M-2).

## 6. Open questions (unresolved at 2026-04-13)

- **C-1 in adversarial venues.** The Suchman register costs us with reviewers who read it as un-rigorous or essayistic. Should C-1 be downgraded to "preferred but suspendable" for top-tier ML venues even when the paper's content is identity-RE?
- **C-4 in non-IS publications.** The Baird shape is calibrated to IS venues. For an HCI or CSCW submission, does the five-element model still apply, or does CSCW's contribution structure (situated study → analytic move → implication) substitute?
- **Internal tension between C-2 and C-3.** Bacon's sentence-level rules sometimes pull against Sexton's arc-level rules (e.g., a Sexton-mandated cause-effect bridge may require a sentence pattern Bacon flags as monotonous). When they conflict, which wins? Currently undecided; surface to user when encountered.
- **Internal tension between C-7 and C-1.** When the author's natural voice is *not* first-person but the venue (and thus C-1) is Suchman-friendly, a C-1 rewrite toward first-person navigation can read as a C-7 idiolect-flattening. Which yields? Currently undecided; surface to user. Related: when no prior accepted prose exists for the author, what supplies the C-7 idiolect baseline? Current fallback is the least-revised passages of the current draft, which is weak for a first draft.

They are separable but co-located; when a single sentence must serve both, which governs the rewrite? Currently: treat as independent findings, surface both.
