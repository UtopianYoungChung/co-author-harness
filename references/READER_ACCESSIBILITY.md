# Reader-Accessibility Constraint — Policy and Operational Criteria

*Extracted from `Ph.D. Research/CLAUDE.md` §13 on 2026-04-22 to reduce always-loaded context overhead. The binding rule is Hard Constraint #8 in the root CLAUDE.md §9. This file is the policy reference; read it when authoring or debugging Check 8 findings.*

---

## 13.1 The constraint, as a thematic claim

**Every revision the harness produces must be accessible to a careful human reader at first read, regardless of the conceptual difficulty of the material on the page.** Accessibility is a property of the prose surface, not of the underlying argument; an intellectually dense piece can be — and at this portfolio's quality bar, must be — fluent, low-friction, and metabolizable in one sitting. The cognitive-load tax a draft imposes on a reader is the Generator's responsibility to keep low; the Evaluator audits the surface; the Reflector logs recurrence.

This constraint applies uniformly across P0 (open exploration), P1 (positioning / contestation), and P2 (resolution / synthesis). A P2 paper is not licensed to be denser at the sentence level because its conclusions are sharper; a P0 paper is not excused from clarity because its argument is provisional. The intellectual difficulty of the work resides in the ideas; the prose carries them, it does not perform them.

Accessibility operates on **two temporal scales** the harness must audit separately. The *local* scale is the paragraph, the sentence pair, the section opening — the text the reader processes within a single reading turn. The *cumulative* scale is the manuscript as a whole — the constructs, positions, and tensions the reader has been asked to carry in working memory since the beginning of the piece. A manuscript can pass every local check (paragraph cadence, sentence rhythm, first-use definition, section signposting, jargon discipline, worked examples at density spikes) and still impose a prohibitive cumulative tax when a reader who has acquired six framings and three positions across twenty pages reaches a seventh-section argument that depends on them all without any intervening accumulation summary. The two scales require distinct operational criteria; the v0.7.4 revision expands §13.3 from six local criteria to seven (adding consolidation anchors as the cumulative-scale criterion) and §13.5 maps the seven to SAFEGUARD Check 8 Sub-checks A–G correspondingly.

## 13.2 The distinction the constraint depends on

Sweller's cognitive-load taxonomy separates **intrinsic load** (the irreducible difficulty of the material), **extraneous load** (friction the prose adds without contributing to understanding), and **germane load** (the effort the reader spends building the mental model). Accessibility minimizes extraneous load while preserving — and at the right moments amplifying — germane load. It does not collapse intrinsic load. A passage that defines `intentionality` precisely on first use, repeats the construct under a consistent name, signposts where the reader is in the argument, and uses sentence rhythm to mark turn-points has lowered extraneous load without trivializing the construct. A passage that mints a synonym every paragraph, embeds nested parenthetical glosses, and chains four subordinate clauses through a dependent verb has spent the reader's working memory on prose-bookkeeping rather than on the idea.

The taxonomy itself implies the two-scale audit named in §13.1. Extraneous load is typically a local phenomenon: a nested parenthetical or an undefined neologism adds friction inside the paragraph that contains it. Germane load, by contrast, is inherently cumulative — the mental model the reader is building is the model that must still be in place when the argument's closing move arrives. Once a manuscript has accumulated enough distinct constructs, positions, or tensions, the germane-load task of holding them all in working memory shades into an extraneous-load problem if the prose does not intermittently consolidate them. The Generator's surface task is local; the manuscript's cumulative-load profile is the Evaluator's audit target. Consolidation anchors — one-sentence restatements at structural boundaries that name the accumulated constructs and signal how the next movement will build on them — are the minimum-cost corrective, additive to rather than substituting for the local checks.

## 13.3 Operational criteria the Evaluator audits at Ph2 and above

The Evaluator runs the following surface checks at Ph2 entry and re-runs them at Ph3 and Ph4. Failures are MAJOR by default and BLOCKER on resubmission if a prior round flagged them.

The criteria are:

- **Paragraph cadence.** No paragraph in `manuscript/main.md` runs longer than roughly 150 words without an internal turn-point (a transition, a worked example, a counter-claim, or a thematic refocus). Paragraphs that span a single argumentative move should not exceed 200 words. Density without cadence is the failure mode the constraint targets.
- **Sentence-length distribution.** A passage in which the average sentence length exceeds 28 words and the standard deviation is below 6 reads as monotone-dense; the Evaluator flags it. Variation between short and long is the carrier of rhythm; rhythm is the carrier of comprehension.
- **First-use definition.** Every theoretical or domain construct introduced into the manuscript carries a first-use definition or worked illustration before it is allowed to do conceptual work in subsequent paragraphs. This is binding even on terms the author considers field-standard (`affordance`, `operationalization`, `socio-technical`, `intentionality`, `delegation`).
- **Signposting at section transitions.** Every section opens with a one-to-three-sentence preamble that tells the reader where they have arrived in the argument and what the section will contribute. This is not a chapter summary; it is a map fragment.
- **Jargon discipline within paragraphs.** A paragraph may not introduce more than two new domain terms. If the argument requires more, the paragraph should be split.
- **Worked examples at density spikes.** Where the conceptual density rises (a tri-part decomposition, a multi-criteria evaluation, a contested-claim cluster), the prose should turn to a worked example, a vignette, or a concrete instantiation before continuing in the abstract. The Generator's `loan-officer vignette` in INF3001H is the model; no project is exempt from the move.
- **Consolidation anchors at structural boundaries (cumulative scale).** Across the manuscript, every major structural boundary that crosses a construct-accumulation threshold must carry a one-sentence consolidation anchor that (a) names the distinct constructs, positions, or tensions the reader has acquired up to that point and (b) signals how the next movement will build on them. The construct-accumulation threshold is met when the prior spans have introduced three or more load-bearing constructs, or when the next section's argument depends on two or more prior sections' material without which the argument does not land. The canonical anchor form is "At this point in the paper, [the reader holds X, Y, Z]; the next movement [does W with them]," though any sentence performing the same two functions is acceptable. Consolidation anchors are **additive** to section-transition signposting (the local fifth criterion), not substitutes for it: signposting orients the reader to the section ahead; anchors consolidate the manuscript behind. A manuscript under roughly 3,000 words rarely meets the threshold; beyond roughly 5,000 words, the absence of anchors is a reliable indicator of cumulative-load failure regardless of local-scale compliance. This is the only §13.3 criterion scoped to the full manuscript rather than to a paragraph or section opening.

These criteria sit alongside — they do not replace — the existing in-package style commitments (`bacon_2009_well_crafted_sentence_guidelines.md` for sentence craft, `Sexton_Fiction_to_Academic_Writing_Guide.md` for narrative arc, `baird_2021_writing_guidelines.md` for IS-discipline conventions, `suchman_writing_style.md` for the asymmetric-design register). The accessibility constraint is the meta-rule those style guides operationalize; it is binding at every P-stage and across every register the portfolio uses.

## 13.4 What accessibility is not

Accessibility is not informality, not the avoidance of theoretical vocabulary, not the suppression of complexity, not the mandate to write to the most generous interpretation. The portfolio is a PhD-level research portfolio; the reader is a careful disciplinary reader, not a lay audience. The constraint binds the Generator to fluency and low extraneous load, not to dilution. A paragraph that sustains a difficult Vidal contradiction-mapping move is fully compliant if its sentences are paced, its constructs are defined, and its rhythm carries the reader; the same paragraph would be non-compliant if it embedded the move inside a 240-word block with three nested parentheticals and an undefined neologism, regardless of how rigorous the underlying argument was.

The Reflector logs recurrence at session close. A pattern of accessibility findings across two or more rounds in a single project is a project-scoped lesson candidate; a pattern across two or more projects becomes an `A5-accessibility-recurrence` plugin-update proposal, routed through the Planner's three-filter gatekeeper (evidence-adequacy / non-duplication / tier-appropriateness) before it ever reaches the user. The audit itself runs in Reflector full mode only, in the net-new **Phase 2g** described at §13.5.

## 13.5 How §13 is enforced under v0.7.4

v0.7.2 lifted this section from a policy statement to an enforced convergence gate; v0.7.4 renames its enforcement surface from T2/T3/T4 to Ph2/Ph3/Ph4 without changing the aggregation or severity mechanics, and the 2026-04-23 revision expands the enforcement surface from six to seven operational criteria (adding the cumulative-scale consolidation-anchor criterion in §13.3). The seven §13.3 operational criteria map one-to-one to SAFEGUARD Check 8 Sub-checks A–G: Cadence-Flag (A), Rhythm-Flag (B), First-Use-Flag (C), Signpost-Flag (D), Jargon-Density-Flag (E), Worked-Example-Flag (F), and Consolidation-Anchor-Flag (G). Sub-checks A–F are **local-scale** and run at the section scope the Evaluator has been dispatched against; Sub-check G is **cumulative-scale** and runs at full-manuscript scope only, which restricts its binding engagement to Ph3 and Ph4 (Ph2 review is section-scoped and therefore cannot see the manuscript-level construct accumulation Sub-check G audits — at Ph2 the check emits advisory findings only). The Evaluator runs the deterministic pre-filter (`DETERMINISTIC_CHECKS §9b`: `cadence_flag_count`, `signpost_flag_count`, `jargon_density_flag_count`) and the judgment overlay (`skills/accessibility-overlay`) at Ph2 (advisory subset with BLOCKER-CANDIDATE tagging), at Ph3 (full severity; **binding at the TerminalSignoffRow**), and at Ph4 (CLEAN required). Aggregation rule (unchanged across the A–F → A–G expansion): one MAJOR → BORDERLINE; two MAJORs → MAJOR; any BLOCKER → BLOCKER. A BLOCKER at Ph3 causes the Planner to refuse the terminal signoff with `E-Ph3-ACCESSIBILITY-BLOCKER-AT-SIGNOFF` and emit ledger trigger 28 `ph3_accessibility_blocker_surfaced`; the refused write does not consume iteration budget. A BORDERLINE verdict permits signoff but propagates the `[CONVERGENCE-BORDERLINE-ACCESSIBILITY]` advisory into `notes`, which Reflector Phase 2g then audits for cross-round recurrence. Style commitment **C-5** in `references/STYLE_COMMITMENTS.md` promotes this rule to a package-tier commitment that cannot be suspended via the §4 relaxation procedure: severity floors may be adjusted project-by-project in `research_notes/directives.md`, and a project may opt in to earlier enforcement (e.g., `D-NN: Accessibility gate enabled from Ph2`), but wholesale opt-out is not permitted.

**Sub-check G advisory-until scoping (2026-04-23 transition).** Because Sub-check G is introduced mid-cycle — after the INF3006Y canonical manuscript entered its P0 stability lock on 2026-04-22 and after the Co Author test variant reached Ph4 — the check runs with an `advisory_until: next_manuscript_at_ph3` flag on any section ledger whose `current_phase` row exceeds Ph2 at the moment of the 2026-04-23 introduction. Under the flag, Sub-check G findings are recorded in `reviews/safeguard_check8_<date>.md` with their severities and locators, but the Planner's §3.3.3 accessibility gate reads only the A–F aggregate for the TerminalSignoffRow decision; a Sub-check G BLOCKER on a flagged section does not fire `E-Ph3-ACCESSIBILITY-BLOCKER-AT-SIGNOFF`. The flag is project-scoped and retires automatically on the first Ph3 entry of any manuscript whose Ph1 classification postdates 2026-04-23; at that point Sub-check G joins A–F at full severity without further action. The one-time advisory scope is recorded here rather than in the section ledger to avoid polluting the 15-field `SectionStateObject` with a transition-only field; the Evaluator reads this §13.5 paragraph at Ph3/Ph4 entry and consults `reviews/classification.md` for the Ph1 classification date to decide whether the flag still applies.
