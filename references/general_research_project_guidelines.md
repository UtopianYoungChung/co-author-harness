# General Guidelines for Research Paper Development

This document outlines a generalized, milestone-driven framework for developing a sustained academic research paper. It is abstracted from successful, structured course assignments and provides a robust path from initial ideation to a final manuscript.

**Normative lifecycle contract.** This document explains the default intellectual progression. `MILESTONE_FEEDBACK_HANDOFF_PROTOCOL.md` is authoritative for deliverable roles, feedback provenance, approval, lineage, exact-byte bindings, reopening, and handoff evidence. Milestones M1-M5 govern project deliverables and accepted handoffs; phases Ph1-Ph4 independently govern revision/readiness.

## Overview of the Milestone Structure
A strong research paper is not produced in a single draft. It requires progressively developing an intellectual "itch" into a clear tension, surveying a contested landscape of literature, and structuring an argument. 

The process is divided into five core milestones:
1. Project Memo
2. Annotated References
3. Structured Outline
4. Paper Draft
5. Final Paper

---

## Milestone 1: Project Memo
**Purpose:** To crystallize the preliminary focus, identify the core problem/tension, isolate problematic assumptions, and establish concrete research questions.

**Key Components:**
- **Focus & Framing:** Define the exact phenomenon being studied. Explicitly distance yourself from buzzwords or industry labels, focusing instead on the *underlying problem* (e.g., shifts in power, changes in work processes, historical continuities).
- **Core Tension / What the Corpus Says:** Identify a clash in the literature. Do authors disagree on definitions, scale of analysis, or where agency/control resides? Read sources side by side to map disagreements.
- **Terms to Hold at Arm's Length:** List specific concepts or jargon that smuggle in hidden implications or assumptions (e.g., "hybrid," "emergence"). Interrogate what each term presupposes before adopting it.
- **Research Questions:** Formulate 2-3 specific, analytical questions that the paper will answer to resolve or illuminate the tension.
- **Snowball Strategy:** Identify the core literature and map outward. Establish tracks for tracing the genealogy of theoretical concepts, the contemporary debate, and the historical precedents.

## Milestone 2: Annotated References
**Purpose:** To actively integrate your sources into your argument rather than merely summarizing them.

**Key Components:**
- **Strategic Summaries:** For each core source, write a brief annotation.
- **Contribution to Tension:** Explicitly state *how* the source informs your research questions. Does it provide a micro-level view? A macro-structural view? A posthumanist critique? 
- **Method of Organization:** Use reference management software (like Zotero) to categorize literature logically based on the "tracks" identified in your snowball strategy.

## Milestone 3: Structured Outline
**Purpose:** To lay bare the skeleton of the argument before committing to prose.

**Key Components:**
- **Abstract & Introduction:** Define the phenomenon, the problem statement, and the exploratory aim.
- **Contested Meanings / Definitions:** Address the definitions of the core concepts. Highlight where practical application clashes with academic theory.
- **Historical Arc:** Connect the modern phenomenon to historical precedents. Delineate what is genuinely new (discontinuities) from what is a recurring challenge (continuities).
- **Core Themes as Comparative Problems:** Break down the analysis into specific axes (e.g., Micro vs. Macro, Relational vs. Structural).
- **Critical Review of Frameworks:** Critique the methodological lenses themselves. Unpack the hidden assumptions of the theories you are borrowing.
- **Synthesis:** Gather the unresolved questions and outline criteria for future analysis.
- **Conclusion:** Summarize the landscape surveyed without necessarily forcing a premature, unified verdict.

## Milestone 4: Paper Draft
**Purpose:** To translate the outline into a complete, flowing manuscript intended for peer or advisor feedback.

**Key Components:**
- **Full Argument Execution:** The draft should reflect the entire argument from start to finish, even if some sections remain provisional.
- **Attributive Voice:** Maintain an analytic, attributive tone ("Author X argues..." or "One framework suggests..."). Avoid evaluative or deterministic language unless explicitly defending a stance.
- **Synthesis over Summary:** Ensure that sections comparing literature actively synthesize the tension rather than providing a sequential list of book reports.

## Milestone 5: Final Paper
**Purpose:** To polish the argument based on feedback and formalize the research output.

**Key Components:**
- **Elaboration:** Fleshing out underdeveloped sections identified during the draft review.
- **Tone & Adherence Check:** Ensuring the paper fulfills all intended research questions and adheres to the planned scope discipline (e.g., remaining an analytical survey rather than a metaphysical manifesto).
- **Final Formatting & Presentation:** Preparing the document for submission and/or accompanying oral presentation reflecting on the intellectual journey.

## Feedback and Handoff Discipline

Each milestone produces its named deliverable, passes an explicit feedback and adjudication gate, receives approval, and emits hash-bound F9 evidence for the next milestone. Planner checks, user/advisor feedback, Evaluator findings, and retrospective applications retain their real provenance; they are not interchangeable labels.

A plan, gate, checklist, review, report, generated view, or export may support or block progress but cannot replace the milestone deliverable. In particular, M4 is the complete manuscript at review-ready depth and M5 is the final manuscript at submission-bound depth. Acceptance of M4 or M5 certifies exact manuscript bytes; later byte changes stale the prior approval and require revalidation under `MILESTONE_FEEDBACK_HANDOFF_PROTOCOL.md`.
