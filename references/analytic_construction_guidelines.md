# Analytic-Construction Guidelines (C-8 substrate)

**Status.** Reference substrate for style commitment **C-8 (analytic-construction discipline)**. Grounds the commitment in a single sustained exemplar: Andrew Abbott, *The System of Professions: An Essay on the Division of Expert Labor* (Chicago: University of Chicago Press, 1988). Full-text extract verified character-for-character against the uploaded source. Rationale memo: `docs/analysis/2026-07-01_abbott-system-of-professions-analytic-construction-and-C8-rationale.md`.

**Provenance and edition note.** The harness already absorbed Abbott once — *Methods of Discovery* and *Digital Paper*, under C-7 (`voice_preservation_guidelines.md`). Those are Abbott **advising on** craft. This file absorbs a different Abbott: the 1988 monograph in which he **performs** the craft. C-7 protects the writer's idiolect; **C-8 governs the writer's argumentative moves** — how a theoretical claim is built, paragraph by paragraph. The two do not overlap: C-7 is protective (what prose must not lose), C-8 is convergent (what a theory-building passage should do), and both cite Abbott without collision.

---

## 1. The layer C-8 occupies

The existing convergent commitments partition the prose into layers, and one layer has no defender:

| Layer | Commitment | Authority | Scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Word / clause | C-2 | Bacon | sentence craft, cadence, rhythm |
| **Analytic move** | **C-8** | **Abbott 1988** | **how a single claim is won: definition, dissolution, demonstration, verdict** |
| Whole-piece arc | C-3 | Sexton | the red thread through the manuscript |
| Theory anatomy | C-4 | Baird | the five-element static shape of the theory |
| Register / voice | C-1 / C-7 | Suchman / Moran et al. | how the prose sounds; what voice must not be lost |

C-4 (Baird) audits whether the theory **has** the right parts — a phenomenon, an artefact, a mechanism, an outcome, a contribution. It does not audit whether those parts were **earned**. A manuscript can satisfy the five-element model with every key term stipulated by fiat, every rival position merely contradicted rather than dissolved, and every claim asserted rather than demonstrated. C-8 governs that middle layer: the **rhetoric of the analytic move** — the unit between the sentence (Bacon) and the arc (Sexton), and between the theory's anatomy (Baird) and its voice (Suchman). Abbott's 1988 monograph is the working exemplar of that layer.

## 2. The seven moves (M-1 … M-7)

Each move is stated, grounded in a verified quotation, and given an operational test. The moves are **convergent targets**, not laws; §5 records their relaxation.

### M-1 — Definitional deferral (definition follows theory, not the reverse)

Abbott refuses to define his central term until the theory forces the definition into being, then derives it from the questions rather than stipulating it up front.

> "Definitions, then, must follow from theoretical questions." — Abbott 1988, ch. 1
> "My central questions and my framework thus determine my definition of profession."

**Why it is a craft move, not a formality.** A stipulated definition pre-empts the argument: it smuggles the conclusion into the premises. A theory-derived definition is falsifiable and shows its work. Abbott makes the derivation visible — the reader watches *abstraction* become the criterion because the competitive theory needs it, not because a dictionary supplied it.

**Operational test.** For each load-bearing term, ask: is it introduced by stipulation ("We define X as…", "By X we mean…") or by derivation from a stated question or framework ("The questions imply the loose definition that…")? Stipulation of a **contested** central construct, with no derivation, is an M-1 failure. A term *imported* by a settled external citation (e.g., a moral-responsibility term taken from a standard source) is not itself an M-1 failure, but the piece should still show *why the case forces this import* rather than a neighbouring one.

**Interaction with C-6 (the upfront-glossary tension).** A project running C-6's "one word, one meaning" discipline will often front-load a terminological glossary — and an upfront glossary is, on its face, the M-1 anti-pattern (stipulation before derivation). The two commitments are reconcilable, and the reconciliation is a rule: **a terminological-consistency glossary is M-1-compatible if it carries definitional *keys only* — the single sense in which each term is used — and defers all *argumentative or derived* content to the body.** The violation is a glossary that pre-announces a *derived* construct's payoff (e.g., defining the very construct the argument exists to earn as "= [the conclusion]"), which deflates the body's derivation by handing the reader the result before the move delivers it. When you see an upfront glossary, do not flag it as an M-1 failure *per se*; flag only the entries that smuggle argumentative content the body is supposed to derive, and name the C-6↔M-1 interaction so the author can adjudicate. (Surfaced by the 2026-07-01 live-run on the QE2026 reciprocity draft; see the rationale memo §5.)

### M-2 — Reconstruct → locate buried assumption → dissolve by moving up a level

Abbott's signature dialectical move: state a rival view **charitably and in full**, find the single unexamined assumption it rests on, and dissolve the dispute by relocating the question one level up rather than by contradicting the rival head-on.

> "The underlying problem is that for many writers, calling something a profession makes it one." — Abbott 1988, ch. 1
> "The contradiction between defining professions by their claims or by their functions is resolved by recalling that the importance of professional social structure lies in its effect on professions' abilities to maintain themselves within a competing system." — ch. 3

**Why.** Head-on contradiction produces a standoff; the reader must pick a side on authority. Dissolution shows the dispute was ill-posed and offers a frame in which it disappears — a stronger and more durable move. The charitable reconstruction is load-bearing: a strawman cannot be dissolved, only knocked over.

**Operational test.** At each point where the manuscript engages a rival position: (a) is the rival stated in a form its own proponents would accept (charity)? (b) is a specific buried assumption named? (c) is the dispute *dissolved* (reframed so it no longer arises) or merely *contradicted* (asserted false)? Contradiction-without-dissolution of a genuinely contested point is an M-2 weakness; strawman reconstruction is an M-2 failure.

### M-3 — Counterexample as demolition

Abbott breaks a rival definition not with argument but with a stubborn concrete case the definition mis-sorts.

> "English barristers do not necessarily train in university but rather by apprenticeship and eating dinners 'in hall.' American clergy do not generally have ethics codes… Yet both groups are unmistakably professions." — Abbott 1988, ch. 1

**Why.** A single well-chosen counterexample does the work of a paragraph of abstract objection, and does it irreversibly: the reader cannot un-see the barrister eating dinners in hall. The concreteness is the mechanism, not decoration.

**Operational test.** Where the manuscript rejects a rival category or criterion, does it supply a **specific** case the rival mis-handles, or only an abstract objection? A rejection resting on abstraction alone, where a counterexample was available, is an M-3 miss. (Note the interaction with C-1/Suchman's "concrete situated example" and C-5's "worked example at density spikes": M-3 is the *argumentative* use of the concrete case; the others are its register and accessibility uses.)

### M-4 — Accumulated parallel instances (anaphoric demonstration)

Abbott converts a general claim into a demonstrated pattern by listing instances in an identical grammatical frame.

> "Thus medicine defeated nursing in the hospital administration area. Thus psychiatry dominated social work and psychology in child guidance. Thus teaching has effectively resisted the efforts of computer scientists to take over classroom teaching." — Abbott 1988, ch. 3

**Why.** The anaphora ("Thus… Thus… Thus…") is not ornament and not monotony — it is the evidence. Three instances in one frame demonstrate the generalization by exhibiting its range. **This is the move most at risk from the existing pipeline:** a mechanical monotony/repetition check (`sentence-level-pass`, Bacon C-2) can read deliberate anaphoric accumulation as a rhythm defect and flag it. C-8 protects the demonstrative anaphora the way C-7 protects repetition-as-signature — see §4.

**Operational test.** Where a general claim is followed by parallel-framed instances, treat the parallelism as **argumentative structure**, not rhythm. Flag it only if the instances do not in fact support the generalization (an evidential defect), never merely because the frame repeats.

### M-5 — The cadential verdict

Abbott builds a long, qualifying, subordinate-clause-laden period and then resolves it with a short declarative that lands the point — the prose equivalent of a cadence.

> "To say a profession exists is to make it one." — Abbott 1988, ch. 3
> "Abstraction enables survival in the competitive system of professions." — ch. 1

**Why.** The short verdict reads as a conclusion **because** it follows accumulated qualification; the rhythm does the persuading. Bacon (C-2 §9.5, "variety and rhythm") notices such sentences as *variety*; C-8 additionally recognizes their **argumentative function** — a verdict sentence resolves a period and closes a move. Deleting it "for concision," or burying it inside the qualifying clause, dissolves the move even when every word survives.

**Operational test.** Does each completed analytic move close on a short, unqualified declarative? A move that trails off into further qualification without a verdict is an M-5 weakness. A "concision" edit that merges the verdict back into the period is an M-5 regression and must cite an independent warrant (cf. the C-7 burden-of-proof rule).

### M-6 — Calibrated confidence (own the judgment; grade it)

Abbott states his confidence unevenly and on the record, marking where a claim is a firm derivation and where it is a judgment call.

> "I am much more confident about my interpretation of professionalism around 1900 than about my interpretation of professionalism now; in principle, the present situation is less knowable." — Abbott 1988, concl.
> "Psychiatry is officially a part of the medical profession, but I have followed what I see as functional reality in calling it a profession here." — ch. 3

**Why.** Graded confidence is an authority move, not a hedge: by naming exactly where he is unsure, Abbott earns trust for the claims he asserts flatly. The first person marks a **decision**, not an opinion. This is where C-8 touches C-1 (Suchman first-person register) — but C-1 governs the *register* of the "I," while C-8 governs its *epistemic calibration*: is confidence graded to the evidence, or uniform regardless of it?

**Operational test.** Are judgment calls marked as such and confidence graded to the evidence, or is every claim asserted at the same flat level? Uniform confidence across claims of visibly different warrant is an M-6 failure (over-claiming on the weak, under-claiming on the strong). Interacts with C-4/Baird's "contribution-claim inflation" — M-6 is the sentence-level discipline that prevents it.

### M-7 — Meta-analytic reflexivity (turn the lens on the instrument)

Abbott interrogates his own analytic apparatus inside the argument that uses it.

> "We must also confront the arbitrary character of our central subjects… historical studies, this one included, make do with relatively strong assumptions about durability and change: essence endures, qualities change. This strategy… must be unmasked." — Abbott 1988, concl.

**Why.** Naming the arbitrariness of one's own categories, while still using them, reads as sophistication rather than weakness — it pre-empts the reviewer's objection by making it first. This is the C-8 analogue of the harness's own `REFLEXIVITY_CHECK.md`: a theory of expert labor that did not examine its own construction would be performatively incoherent.

**Operational test.** For a P2-stage or theory-contribution manuscript, is there at least one place where the apparatus examines its own assumptions (the arbitrariness of the unit of analysis, the aesthetic character of a modeling choice)? Total absence of reflexivity in a theory-building piece is an M-7 gap (advisory below P2; a finding at P2 / theory-contribution).

### The phenomenon-first opening (M-1 support)

Abbott's Preface opens on a situated scene — the Manteno State Hospital ward — and lets the phenomenon raise the question the theory will answer:

> "'In an official sense. . . .' What is it to possess and control expertise?" — Abbott 1988, Preface

This is where M-1 (definitional deferral) and C-3 (Sexton show-then-tell) and C-1 (Suchman situated example) coincide. C-8 reads the opening for whether the **phenomenon drives the theory** or the theory is announced and then illustrated. The former is the Abbott move; the latter is the "definition-first, literature-review-first" cold open C-3 already warns against.

## 3. What C-8 is **not**

- **Not a theory-anatomy check.** C-4 (Baird) audits the five-element static shape. C-8 audits the *dynamics of the move* that assembles those elements. A manuscript can pass C-4 and fail C-8 (well-formed theory, un-earned claims) and vice versa.
- **Not a sentence-craft check.** C-2 (Bacon) owns focus, parallelism, modification, rhythm at the clause level. C-8 borrows Bacon's rhythm vocabulary only to name the *argumentative* function of the cadential verdict (M-5) and to protect demonstrative anaphora (M-4) from a mechanical monotony flag.
- **Not a register or voice check.** C-1 (Suchman) owns the first-person register; C-7 protects idiolect. C-8's M-6 governs the *epistemic calibration* of first-person judgments, not their register or their preservation.
- **Not universal hygiene.** Like C-1…C-4, C-8 is a **methodological commitment** — a defensible stance about how theory should be argued, not neutral correctness. It is suspendable (§5). A venue that wants claims asserted flat and definitions stipulated up front (some formal-methods and systems venues) may legitimately relax it.

## 4. Interaction with C-2 and C-7 (the anaphora / verdict carve-outs)

Two C-8 moves are at risk from mechanical craft checks, and C-8 supplies the carve-out the way C-7 supplies the idiolect carve-out:

- **M-4 demonstrative anaphora vs. C-2 monotony.** When parallel-framed instances follow a general claim (`Thus X… Thus Y… Thus Z…`), the repetition is argumentative structure. `sentence-level-pass` must not flag it as a rhythm defect; it is flagged only if the instances fail to support the claim (an evidential defect the Evaluator, not the sentence pass, owns).
- **M-5 cadential verdict vs. C-2/C-5 "concision."** A short declarative that resolves a long period is doing argumentative work. A concision or accessibility edit that merges it back into the period, or deletes it, must cite an independent warrant — it is otherwise an M-5 regression.

These carve-outs are the mirror image of C-7's: C-7 protects *voice* features from being optimized away; C-8 protects *argumentative* features from the same. Both place the burden of proof on the rule that would strip the feature.

## 5. Relaxation and severity

**Applicability (recorded by the Planner at classification).** C-8 applies by default to any piece that **builds or extends a theory or a conceptual argument** — research papers making a theoretical contribution, thesis theory chapters, P2-stage manuscripts, conceptual sections of applied work, **and advisor-facing conceptual briefs or working drafts toward a proposal that stake a theoretical position** (even when the genre label is "memo" or "brief"). The genre label does not decide applicability; the *presence of a built or extended conceptual argument* does. It is **not** applied by default to: pure methods notes, logistics or status memos, empirical-results sections that report rather than argue, response letters, or P0/P1-stage phenomenon-collection pieces (where the P2 moves are premature — see the P-stage note below). A conceptual brief that deliberately *holds positions open* (P1 non-convergence) is in scope but audited only on the stage-neutral moves M-3/M-5/M-6. *(Carve-in added 2026-07-01 after a live-run showed a substantive supervision brief falling through the "memo" exclusion by reviewer judgment rather than by rule.)*

**P-stage gating.** M-1 (definitional deferral), M-2 (dissolution), and M-7 (reflexivity) are **P2-stage moves** — they presuppose a research problem sharp enough to argue. Firing them against a P1 characterization piece is itself a stage error (cf. `p-stage-checker`). At P0/P1, C-8 is limited to M-3 (counterexample), M-5 (verdict), and M-6 (calibrated confidence), which are stage-neutral. This gating prevents C-8 from pushing a phenomenon-collection paper to over-claim.

**Suspension.** C-8 is suspended via `research_notes/directives.md` (§4 relaxation procedure of `STYLE_COMMITMENTS.md`) when a venue or house style requires flat assertion or up-front stipulation. Example: `D-NN: For project X's systems-venue submission, suspend M-1 (definitional deferral) — the venue expects a stipulated definitions table in §2.` Individual moves may be suspended without suspending the whole commitment.

**Severity.** M-1 stipulation of a contested central construct, M-2 strawman reconstruction, and M-6 uniform over-claiming are **[MAJOR]** candidates. M-2 contradiction-without-dissolution, M-3 missed counterexample, M-5 missing verdict, and M-7 absent reflexivity (at P2) are **[MINOR]**. Below P2, all M-7 findings are ADVISORY. Every C-8 finding names the move (`[MAJOR — C-8/M-1: contested construct stipulated, not derived]`) so the author can dispute the commitment or the finding.

## 6. Reflexive coda — Abbott's substance as a lens on the harness itself

*The System of Professions* is not only a craft exemplar; its **content** is a theory of how a division of expert labor allocates jurisdiction — and the harness *is* a division of expert labor (Generator, Evaluator, Reflector, Planner, and the skills, each with a claimed competence over the manuscript). Abbott's substantive apparatus therefore reads back onto the architecture. This coda records the mapping; the operational proposal lives in the rationale memo §6 and is **recorded, not yet ratified**.

- **Jurisdiction is claimed in three arenas** — legal (formal rules), public (professional rhetoric), and workplace (what actually happens on the job). The harness's analogues: the **legal** claim is `AGENT_CONTRACTS.md` and the invariants; the **public** claim is `README.md` / `SKILL_REGISTRY.md`; the **workplace** is runtime dispatch. Abbott's central empirical finding — that workplace jurisdiction routinely drifts from legal claims ("workplace assimilation") — predicts a specific harness pathology: an agent or skill whose *runtime* behavior has quietly drifted from its *contract*. That is a diagnosable condition, and the memo proposes it as a reflection-time audit.
- **Settlements are not only full conquest.** Abbott catalogues subordination, division of labor, an advisory relation, and division by client. The harness currently resolves inter-skill disputes by **fixed precedence** (the ladder) — i.e., it recognizes only subordination. Abbott's catalogue suggests the missing settlements: two skills may hold **divided** jurisdiction over one finding (M-4 anaphora is C-8's, its evidential adequacy is the Evaluator's — a division, not a precedence), or an **advisory** relation (a craft skill advises but does not bind the Generator). Naming these makes current ad-hoc overlaps legible.
- **Vacancies pull in claimants.** Abbott: jurisdictions open when a task is left unclaimed. C-8 exists because an analytic-move vacancy sat between C-2 and C-4 with no claimant, and features in a vacancy "get optimized away" (the exact argument C-7 made for idiolect). The reflexive point: the harness should expect its own coverage vacancies to be filled by whatever adjacent check is nearest — usually a mechanical one that mis-handles the vacated task (Bacon flagging Abbott's anaphora). Auditing for such vacancies is a standing reflection task.

This coda is deliberately bounded: it licenses a **diagnostic vocabulary** and one proposed audit, not a rearchitecture. Abbott's own warning (M-7) applies to it — the jurisdiction metaphor is a modeling choice whose arbitrariness must stay visible, and it is offered here as a lens, not a law.
