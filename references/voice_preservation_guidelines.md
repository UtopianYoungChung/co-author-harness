# Voice-Preservation Guidelines — the substrate for Commitment C-7

**Status.** Read by the Generator before any prose-shaping action that could touch idiolect, by the Evaluator when running `SAFEGUARD_LAYER.md` Check 6, and by the Planner when recording whether C-7 applies. Grounds `STYLE_COMMITMENTS.md` C-7 (authorial voice-fingerprint preservation). Absorbed under the v0.17.0 source-absorption pattern (`references/<source>_guidelines.md` + binding hooks).

**Provenance.** Extracted 2026-06-30 from four craft authorities supplied by the author — Moran, *First You Write a Sentence*; Zinsser, *On Writing Well* (30th anniv.); Strunk, *The Elements of Style* (original 1918/1921, De A'Morelli Classic Edition); Abbott, *Methods of Discovery* and *Digital Paper* — with corroboration from Turabian ch. 11 and the Penguin Writer's Manual "Good style" chapter. ## 1. The governing distinction: mechanics vs. identity

All four craft books converge on one split, and C-7 is built on it:

- **Mechanics layer (rules legitimately operate).** Clutter, error, vagueness, dead metaphor, broken parallelism, cliché, genuine comprehension defects. Strunk's "omit needless words," Zinsser's pruning, Bacon's sentence craft, the Blue Book's correctness — all valid here.
- **Identity layer (rules destroy what they touch).** Sentence-length signature, repetition tolerance, point of view (the "I"), cadence/rhythm, humor, evaluative stance, characteristic metaphor. This is "the smell of the writer."

The rule of thumb: **mechanics serve identity; they never overwrite it.** A style edit that touches the identity layer must cite an independent mechanics or accessibility (C-5) defect, or it is taste smuggled in as a fix (cf. `SAFEGUARD_LAYER.md` Check 5).

## 2. Idiolect-bearing features (the C-7 non-target list)

These features are the author's fingerprint. They are **not targets for "improvement"** absent an independently established content or accessibility defect. Each carries a grounding quotation.

| Feature | Why it carries voice | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Sentence-length signature** | "When you vary the length of your sentences… your writing will, as if by magic, fill with life and voice." | Moran, "The High-wire Act" |
| **Average, not maximum, length** | "there is nothing wrong with long sentences per se. Average sentence length, not some arbitrary maximum, is what counts. Long sentences and long words are fine so long as they bump up against short ones." | Moran |
| **Repetition tolerance** | "How much a writer tolerates repetition comprises a key part of his voice." | Moran (David Peace / DFW contrast) |
| **Point of view / the "I"** | "If you aren't allowed to use 'I,' at least think 'I' while you write." | Zinsser, "The Sound of Your Voice" |
| **One voice across subjects** | "Don't alter your voice to fit your subject… Develop one voice that readers will recognize." | Zinsser |
| **Cadence / rhythm / sound** | the "wave in the mind"; "sound of sense"; voice "comes alive through word order." | Moran |
| **Contractions, conversational register** | "Your style will be warmer and truer to your personality if you use contractions." | Zinsser |
| **Humor / crotchets** | "If it amuses you in the act of writing, put it in… only you can put it in." | Zinsser |
| **Evaluative stance / intellectual personality** | "your intellectual personality… decisively influence[s]… everything about the way you think"; "every aspect of your intellectual character… is both a strength and a weakness." | Abbott, *Methods of Discovery* |
| **Characteristic metaphor & affect** | "your cranky guide"; waxing "romantic about libraries"; "a distinctive kind of book." | Abbott, *Digital Paper* |

**C-7 protects disciplined idiolect, not every recurring weakness.** Recurrence is necessary but not sufficient for protection. A feature qualifies as idiolect only if it is (a) *recurrent* in the baseline, (b) *not* a comprehension defect (it does not breach C-5 accessibility), and (c) plausibly *intentional* — a signature the author would defend, not an unexamined habit. A recurring genuine defect is still a defect: a mechanical error repeated across the baseline (a consistent its/it's confusion, a dangling-modifier habit), an LLM tic that survived earlier rounds ("not X but Y" stacking, triadic-list reflex, hedged transitions at every break — see `MASTER §A.4.2`), or clutter the author repeats out of inattention is **not** shielded by C-7. The test: *would the author, shown this feature, claim it as voice or concede it as a slip?* When that is unclear — especially under a provisional baseline — flag it for the author's judgment rather than silently protecting or silently removing it. C-7 raises the burden of proof on a rewrite; it does not grant amnesty to recurring error.

## 3. The anti-patterns C-7 guards against

1. **Readability-score optimization.** Moran: a word processor "scored your writing for 'readability' and told you… that you are done"; "A sentence written solely via some slide-rule calculus of readability will never be quite good enough." → **No rule may target a readability number.** Average-length and comprehension are the only sanctioned length signals (and C-5 owns comprehension).
2. **Plain-style-as-neutrality.** Moran: "The plain style is just that — a style." → Mandating a transparent/impersonal register is a *position*, not hygiene; it is C-1's borrowed register, suspendable, not a default that overrides the author.
3. **Per-subject re-registering.** Zinsser: "Don't alter your voice to fit your subject." → Switching borrowed registers (Suchman/Bacon/Vidal) per piece is a relaxable choice, never silent default.
4. **Editing out the writer before they appear.** Zinsser's first-paragraph test: "start with the paragraph where the writer begins to sound like himself or herself." → Do not amputate an opening's voice in an early mechanical pass.
5. **Formula over individual thought.** Strunk: write "your own individual thought, and… refuse to be satisfied with a ready-made formula." → Even economy rules defend idiolect against canned phrasing; they do not impose a register.
6. **Pigeonholing the analysis.** Abbott: wording is "an arbitrary — even an aesthetic — decision"; the "pigeonholer" deforms things "to get them into classifiable shape." → A rewrite to a target register overrides the author's selection and emphasis, which *is* the analysis.

## 4. What C-7 still endorses (the mechanics layer is untouched)

C-7 is **not** a license for sloppy or self-indulgent prose. All four authors are disciplinarians on the mechanics layer:

- Strunk's Rule 13 (omit needless words; "make every word tell") stands.
- Zinsser's pruning and simplicity stand — they *uncover* voice ("Trying to add style is like adding a toupee"); the mechanical act reveals the self rather than imposing a register.
- Bacon (C-2), Sexton (C-3), Baird (C-4), Blue Book mechanics, and the Grounding Protocol all stand.
- Penguin "Good style": "The rules of style are not intended as a means of stifling self-expression, but as a means of enhancing it." Turabian ch. 11: choose active/passive "by considering which gives you the right kind of subject," and first person is permitted "to make writing more lively and personal."

The discipline is real; what C-7 forbids is letting that discipline cross into the identity layer without a content warrant.

## 5. Operationalization (how the pipeline consumes this file)

1. **Establish an idiolect baseline — and grade its confidence.** Before scoring voice, sample the author's signature from (in priority order) prior accepted prose for this author/project, then the least-revised passages of the current draft. Record the sentence-length distribution, repetition habits, point of view, and characteristic devices. (`SAFEGUARD_LAYER.md` Check 6, idiolect-baseline step.) **The baseline's authority is tiered by its source:**
   - **Confident baseline** — drawn from *accepted prior prose* by this author (a published piece, an approved chapter, prose the author has signed off). C-7 findings carry full force: idiolect-flattening is `[MAJOR — C-7]`, and the craft-skill carve-out fully suppresses a matching flag.
   - **Provisional baseline** — drawn only from the *least-revised passages of the current draft* (no accepted prior prose exists; common on a first draft). Treat every C-7 claim as provisional: emit idiolect-flattening at **[MINOR]/ADVISORY**, not [MAJOR], and do **not** fully suppress a craft flag — instead **note** it ("possible baseline idiolect; baseline is provisional — confirm with the author"). A provisional baseline is a hypothesis about the author's voice, not evidence of it. Escalate to full force only once accepted prose is available or the author confirms the pattern.
2. **Score drift against the baseline first, the register second.** A "voice degraded" finding must say whether the prose drifted from *this author* (a real C-7 defect) or merely from the borrowed exemplar (often acceptable; possibly a C-1 matter the author can dispute).
3. **Apply the idiolect carve-out in craft skills.** A monotony/passive/melodrama pattern that recurs in the baseline and is not a comprehension defect is **idiolect → report as strength/[INFO]**, not [MINOR].
4. **Invert the burden of proof on style rewrites.** The Generator may not strip an identity-layer feature on taste alone; the edit must cite a correctness, clutter, or C-5 accessibility warrant. This is the style-side parallel of the Grounding Protocol's no-fabrication rule.

## 6. Open fronts (unresolved 2026-06-30)

- **Baseline source.** What supplies the idiolect baseline when no prior accepted prose exists? The least-revised current-draft fallback is now handled by the **provisional-baseline tier** (§5.1: C-7 claims drop to [MINOR]/ADVISORY, craft flags noted not suppressed), but that is a severity discipline, not a real signal — a stronger first-draft baseline source remains open.
- **C-7 vs. C-1.** When the author's natural voice is *not* first-person but the venue is Suchman-friendly, which yields? Surface to the user (parallel to `STYLE_COMMITMENTS.md §6` C-2/C-3 tension).
- **Deterministic extraction.** Whether baseline extraction eventually warrants its own script alongside `d_style_profile_check.py`. Declined at introduction per scope.
- **Gate mode for C-7-sensitive findings.** `run_all.py --fail-on any` still hard-fails on advisory craft/voice/length findings, which are C-7 candidates that need an author-baseline read no deterministic pass can perform. The v0.19.0 patch only documents this in the `--fail-on` help text (light touch, no behavior churn). A stronger future step: a gate mode that excludes C-7-sensitive style findings from the fail set (e.g., `--fail-on blocking`, or a category filter so only mechanics/grounding findings can gate). Recorded, not implemented — implementing it changes the audit script's exit-code contract and should be a deliberate, separately reviewed change.
