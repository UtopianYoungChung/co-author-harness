# Advisory Response: Highest Residual Risk and Single Mitigating Move

## The Risk Diagnosis

**P-9 (the Ph3-refine / Ph4-deep split) carries the highest residual risk of regressing the Ph3 convergence guarantee.** Here's why:

Every other proposal in the ship-at-RC bucket is additive or orthogonal — P-10 refines diff-scoping, P-11 adds a dispatch field, P-13 declares an optimization target, P-15 surfaces advisory information, and the register-mismatch patch is a Reflector-side audit. None of these *redefine what "converged" means*. P-9 does. It structurally redefines the Ph3 exit condition by splitting the check surface into two tiers and asserting that a section can be called "Ph3_converged" without having cleared the eight heavier workloads.

The P-9a pre-MCR Ph3-deep pass was added specifically to close the EG-1 loophole (late-discovery BLOCKER on a deferred workload that can't re-enter Ph3-refine). But P-9a introduces its own risk: **it creates a single synchronous gate between a lighter iterative phase and a heavier batch phase, and the convergence guarantee now depends on the completeness of P-9's enumeration of deferred workloads.** If P-9's enumeration is wrong — if a check that should be in the Ph3-refine profile is accidentally classified as Ph4-deep — then sections will reach Ph3_converged with a blind spot, and P-9a catches it only at MCR entry, when the cost of rework is highest.

This is not a hypothetical. The enumeration in P-9 is a design-time classification decision made without empirical validation (the proposal itself acknowledges its evidence base is structural, not empirical). The risk is that the Ph3-refine profile is *under-specified* — that it excludes a check whose findings correlate with refinement-phase edits and therefore should iterate, not batch.

**Why this beats P-12/P-16 as the top risk:** P-12 and P-16 are provisional precisely because their thresholds are unmotivated. But they gate *when* convergence is declared, not *what convergence means*. A wrong P-12 threshold causes too-early or too-late convergence declaration on a correctly-scoped check surface. A wrong P-9 enumeration causes convergence on the *wrong check surface entirely*. The latter is categorically worse.

## The Single Architectural Move

**Add a mandatory P-9 classification-validation step at RC freeze: for each check assigned to Ph4-deep, require a written falsification argument explaining why that check's findings are independent of Ph3-refine iteration edits.**

Concretely: before RC scope freeze, for each of the eight deferred workloads (SAFEGUARD 1/4/5/7, IS-theory pass, Suchman register audit, public-interest accountability, Coupling E.2 overlay, Sexton narrative-structure, abstract-body, full bibliography contradiction sweep), the proposal must state:

1. **What class of Ph3-refine edits could trigger a finding on this check?**
2. **Why those edits are unlikely during Ph3-refine iteration** (or why, if they occur, the finding would be caught by the Ph3-refine profile's existing checks).

If the falsification argument cannot be made for a given check, that check must be promoted back to Ph3-refine or given a lightweight proxy in the Ph3-refine profile.

## Why This Move and Not Another

- It attacks the *classification correctness* of P-9, which is the load-bearing assumption of the entire split.
- It is a one-time gate at RC freeze, not ongoing operational overhead.
- It converts the currently implicit "structural observation" evidence into an explicit, auditable argument per check.
- It is compatible with P-9a (P-9a remains the safety net; the falsification step reduces how much weight P-9a must bear).
- It respects the existing four-agent contract and doesn't require new machinery.

## What It Won't Fix

This move does not address P-10's context-halo incompleteness, P-12/P-16's unmotivated thresholds, or P-14's cross-paragraph contamination. Those are real risks but they don't threaten the convergence guarantee as directly as a misclassified P-9 check surface.
