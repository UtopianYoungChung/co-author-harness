# v0.7.5 Ph3 Refinement-Loop Proposal — Review Response

## 0. Framing

This document is the filed response to the evaluation of `proposals/v0.7.5_phase3_refinement_loop_proposal.md` received in the 2026-04-21 session. The response follows academic-peer-review convention: each reviewer point is cited, given an accept / partial-accept / defer verdict, and coupled to a concrete action against the proposal or its change surfaces. The response does not itself revise the proposal. It records the disposition the revision will carry, and surfaces the resulting scope-change decision for user approval.

The evaluation is treated seriously on its theoretical framing first (§1 of this response), its per-proposal critiques second (§2), and its cross-cutting concerns third (§3). A revised shipping scope is proposed at §4 and a migration-of-the-proposal plan at §5. A user-decision surface sits at §6.

Three framing observations before the point-by-point.

First, the evaluation's §4.1 self-gatekeeping critique is correct and methodologically sharp. The proposal's §5 was written by the author of §§0–3, which is precisely the structural weakness the three-filter gatekeeper exists to prevent. The response accepts the critique without qualification and logs Action A-GK-1: route the full package through `advisor-escalation` (the plugin's external-consult skill) before the v0.7.5 RC scope freeze, with the advisor's output re-classified defensively as EXTERNAL per Ph.D.-root Hard Constraint #6, before revising the proposal.

Second, the evaluation's §2 theoretical reading catches a claim the proposal elides: the Generator-Evaluator separation (a two-agent / two-file / two-contract discipline) is stronger than the register-switch-within-one-Evaluator-file discipline P-9 proposes. The response accepts the asymmetry and proposes a concrete soundness patch — a net-new register-audit finding class, parallel in structure to the existing Phase 2f contract audit, logged as Action A-RG-1.

Third, the evaluation's §4.4 observation on advisor-escalation coupling at stall is complementary to §4.1's self-gatekeeping point: the advisor surface is the harness's own mechanism for adversarial independence, and the proposal's Ph3-deep profile does not invoke it. Both points converge on the same action: the advisor pathway is load-bearing under v0.7.5 in ways v0.7.4 did not require, and the v0.7.5 RC cannot ship without routing the package's scope-freeze decision through it.

## 1. Response to §2 (theoretical claims)

### 1.1. Register-switch soundness (reviewer §2, first theoretical claim)

**Reviewer's claim.** The Generator-Evaluator separation works because two agents occupy two files with two contracts and two invariants; P-9 asks for two adversarial registers within a single Evaluator file, which is a structurally weaker discipline. §7's instrumentation measures outcomes of decomposition, not soundness of register-switching. A register-audit finding class, parallel to Phase 2f, would close this gap.

**Response — ACCEPT.**

**Action A-RG-1.** Extend the Reflector's Phase 2f contract audit with a new sub-check, `R-Refl-RG-1 register_mismatch` (severity MAJOR): at Ph3 iteration close, verify that the Evaluator's filed findings match the declared adversarial register for the round's profile. A Ph3-refine round that produces certification-register findings (e.g., IS-theory alignment verdicts on unmutated elements) trips the audit. A Ph4 round that produces refinement-register findings (e.g., sentence-level diffs without submission-readiness framing) also trips it. The audit operates at the F1 frontmatter level (register tag as a new field), not at prose level, so its cost is bounded.

**Action A-RG-2.** Add `adversarial_register: refinement | certification` to the F1 frontmatter schema in `references/ARTEFACT_FRONTMATTER_SCHEMA.md`. The Evaluator declares its register at each dispatch; the Reflector's A-RG-1 audit reads this field.

**Instrumentation amendment to §7.** Add a fifth falsification metric: **register-mismatch rate** — the fraction of Ph3-refine rounds in which A-RG-1 fires. Claim: ≤ 5% across the first twenty v0.7.5 rounds. If the rate exceeds 15%, the register-switch discipline is inadequate and P-9 must be reconsidered as a two-agent rather than a one-agent-two-registers design.

### 1.2. Check-surface invariance framing (reviewer §2, second theoretical claim)

**Reviewer's claim.** §1(a) asserts check-surface invariance as a structural claim, but the invariance is plausible-not-demonstrated. The IS-theory pass's five-element verdict will move if the tightened paragraph anchors an element; the Suchman inventory will move if the citation anchors a move. The structural claim is better read as "many checks are approximately iteration-invariant on most diffs," with P-11 as the tuning knob for exceptions.

**Response — ACCEPT.**

**Action A-FR-1.** Revise §1(a) prose in the proposal to the reviewer's softer formulation. The revised prose acknowledges the approximate-invariance claim, names the anchor-dependence as the exception class, and cites P-11's profile selection as the mechanism for handling exceptions — specifically, the structural-delta flag that escalates from refine to structural when a section-level structural change is detected. An anchor-dependent change (the tightened paragraph carrying an IS-theory element; the re-paginated citation anchoring a Suchman move) is a structural change by the flag's definition and will already escalate.

**Secondary patch.** The evaluation implicitly surfaces a methodological drift: the proposal reaches for "structural" as a framing move where the predecessor reached for "empirical (n=1)". The response acknowledges the drift and marks the §1 evidence base honestly as *structural with empirical backfill deferred to §7's instrumentation* rather than as structural-full-stop. Action A-IN-1 at §3.2 below amends §7 to backfill retrospectively from existing convergence-journal and convergence-log data, closing the methodological gap without pretending to empirical grounding the package does not yet have.

## 2. Response to §3 (proposal-by-proposal)

### 2.1. P-9 / P-11 — pre-MCR Ph3-deep pass (reviewer §3, P-9/P-11)

**Reviewer's claim.** A demoted check that surfaces a Ph4 BLOCKER after MCR admission triggers EG-1 Ph4→Ph3 downgrade, and the Ph3-refine profile cannot address the finding because the relevant check is not in the refine envelope. A pre-MCR Ph3-deep pass, invoked exactly once per manuscript before MCR assembly as a non-negotiable admission requirement, closes the loophole while preserving the wall-clock benefit of refine-default.

**Response — ACCEPT.** The loophole is real and has structural rather than budgetary character. EG-1 already exists to handle Ph4→Ph3 demotion on grounding violation; under v0.7.5 the demotion class widens to any demoted-check BLOCKER, and a re-admitted Ph3 round under refine cannot clear it. The pre-MCR pass is the correct remediation because it surfaces the issue before MCR admission rather than after, converting a post-admission re-entry loop into a pre-admission certification step.

**Action A-MCR-1.** Add a new proposal sub-point **P-9a — Pre-MCR Ph3-deep pass**, formally a refinement to P-9: before the Manuscript Convergence Report is assembled, every section must complete exactly one Ph3-deep pass (not Ph3-refine or Ph3-structural). The pass is non-negotiable, gated at the Planner's MCR-assembly contract, and surfaced as a new field `pre_mcr_deep_pass_completed: bool` in the `SectionStateObject`. A section whose `pre_mcr_deep_pass_completed` is false is refused MCR admission with `E-MCR-PRE-DEEP-PASS-REQUIRED`. The pre-MCR pass counts as a single iteration against the section's budget; the +50% MCR iteration reserve absorbs its cost.

**Action A-MCR-2.** `references/PHASE_PROTOCOL.md §3.4` (MCR admission contract) is amended to enumerate the pre-MCR-deep-pass requirement alongside the existing `Ph3_converged` and non-stale requirements.

**Action A-MCR-3.** The §7 instrumentation adds a sixth falsification metric: **late-discovery EG-1 trigger rate** under v0.7.5 vs. v0.7.4.1 baseline. Claim: the pre-MCR pass reduces EG-1 triggers by the fraction of Ph3 BLOCKERs that the demoted-check classes would have produced. Falsification: if the rate exceeds v0.7.4.1 baseline, the pre-MCR pass is not capturing what it was designed to capture and P-9's boundary has to be retuned.

### 2.2. P-10 — context halo (reviewer §3, P-10)

**Reviewer's claim.** Cross-paragraph checks — contradiction detection, signposting, parts of Check 8 (Signpost-Flag, Worked-Example-Flag) — depend on context that extends beyond the paragraph whose hash changed. Without a context halo, P-14's carryover cache will inherit verdicts from paragraphs whose verdict legitimately changed because a neighbour moved.

**Response — ACCEPT.**

**Action A-HL-1.** Amend P-10's diff-scope rule to include an **immediate-neighbour halo**: a paragraph whose SHA-256 changed admits its immediate preceding and immediate following paragraphs into the judgment scope regardless of their hash state. The halo applies to the judgment layer only; the grounding layer continues to read the full file unconditionally.

**Action A-HL-2.** For cross-paragraph checks with section-level dependence (contradiction detection against the full bibliography; Check 8 Signpost-Flag at section-opening discipline), the halo extends to the full containing section. The distinction between immediate-neighbour and section-wide halo is encoded per-check in `references/DETERMINISTIC_CHECKS.md` and `references/SAFEGUARD_LAYER.md`, surfaced as `halo_scope: paragraph | immediate_neighbour | containing_section` per check.

**Coupling to P-14.** The halo semantics flow downstream into P-14's carryover-cache key. An unchanged paragraph whose immediate neighbour changed is no longer cache-eligible; its verdict must be re-judged. This is captured in Action A-CK-1 at §2.6 below.

### 2.3. P-12 — threshold derivation (reviewer §3, P-12, first concern)

**Reviewer's claim.** The thresholds `|findings_count_delta| ≤ 1` and `|line_delta| ≤ 0.5% of section length` are unmotivated numeric parameters that Grounding Protocol would flag as fabricated in manuscript prose. They are load-bearing — they decide when Ph3 closes. Either derive from v0.7.4.1 retrospective analysis or mark provisional with a v0.7.6 retuning milestone.

**Response — ACCEPT.** This is the sharpest internal-consistency critique in the review: the proposal violates its own discipline by floating numeric parameters free. The derivation is tractable. Both INF3001H and INF3006Y carry existing `reviews/convergence_log.md` rounds (and v0.7.4-vintage `convergence_journal.jsonl` where already split per v0.7.4 P-4).

**Action A-TH-1.** Before the v0.7.5 RC scope freeze, run a retrospective analysis across INF3001H and INF3006Y convergence-journal rows: fit the four-component vector to historical data, and derive the three numeric thresholds — findings_count_delta bound, line_delta bound, and the three-round window — as the values at which the vector would have fired at the rounds that the user retrospectively identifies as "this was the right place to close". Emit the analysis as `proposals/v0.7.5_threshold_derivation.md` with the retrospective fitted values and a short confidence-interval prose paragraph.

**Action A-TH-2.** If the retrospective data is insufficient — fewer than five rounds per project, or no user-identifiable "right-to-close" rounds — mark the thresholds **provisional** in P-12's revised text, schedule their retuning as a v0.7.6 milestone, and extend the `convergence_journal.jsonl` F1-family frontmatter with a `threshold_version: "v0.7.5-provisional"` field so that v0.7.6's retuning can re-derive against rounds tagged under v0.7.5-provisional values.

### 2.4. P-12 — orthogonality (reviewer §3, P-12, second concern)

**Reviewer's claim.** The four components are not orthogonal: `findings_count_delta` and `check8_aggregate` share a Check-8 common cause, and a Ph3 round that drops Check 8 findings will move both signals together. The vector will over-converge in one direction and stall in another.

**Response — PARTIAL ACCEPT.** The correlation concern is valid; the proposal understated it. The partial pushback is that the two components operate on different types: `check8_aggregate` is a categorical verdict (CLEAN / BORDERLINE / MAJOR / BLOCKER) derived from the Check 8 sub-check aggregation rule, while `findings_count_delta` is a numeric count over all Ph3-refine findings across every check class. The correlation is partial, not total, but it is high enough to produce the over-convergence-in-one-direction pattern the reviewer names.

**Action A-OR-1.** Amend P-12's `findings_count_delta` to **exclude Check 8 findings** from the count. The two components are then decorrelated by construction: `check8_aggregate` captures Check 8 state; `findings_count_delta` captures non-Check-8 finding state. The diagnostic-attribution property the reviewer wants is restored: a stall on one component does not mask drift on the other.

**Secondary patch.** Amend the §7 instrumentation to add a **component-correlation statistic** — the Pearson correlation of the four components across rounds — to confirm decorrelation holds in the v0.7.5 replication data. If correlation exceeds 0.5 across any pair, the decorrelation patch is inadequate and P-12's decomposition has to be re-specified.

### 2.5. P-13 — optimization target (reviewer §3, P-13)

**Reviewer's claim.** Three parallel subagents running full-file grounding reads consume 3× the token-substrate, not 1×. The proposal does not specify which budget it optimizes: wall-clock per iteration (user-waiting time) or aggregate compute per round (dollar cost). The two diverge under parallelism.

**Response — ACCEPT.**

**Action A-OT-1.** Declare the optimization target **explicitly as wall-clock per iteration** in P-13's revised text, and frame P-13 honestly as a **cost-for-time trade**. The user is the party who bears the wall-clock cost of iteration (iteration is interactive; the user is waiting on each round), and the package is calibrated around user-waiting time rather than aggregate compute. This is the correct framing given the user directive that drove the proposal — *"the speed and accuracy of each iteration matter"* — which is a wall-clock directive on its face.

**Action A-OT-2.** Add to P-13 a **budget-monitor line-item**: the parallel-dispatch cost (aggregate compute) is logged per round in `convergence_journal.jsonl` under `parallel_dispatch_cost_multiplier: float`, so that the aggregate-compute trade is visible and can be audited at scope-freeze time. If the multiplier exceeds 3.5× consistently, the trade is sharper than the wall-clock gain justifies and P-13 should be revisited — but this is an informed decision, not an accident.

**Action A-OT-3.** Add a **parallel-dispatch opt-out flag** to the F6 dispatch plan. Projects for which aggregate compute is the binding constraint (e.g., a long-running v0.7.6 refinement cycle under a capped budget) can set `parallel_dispatch: false` and fall through to sequential dispatch, trading wall-clock for aggregate compute. Default is `true` under the Ph3-refine profile.

### 2.6. P-14 — cache-key extension (reviewer §3, P-14)

**Reviewer's claim.** Even with P-10's diff-scope and the context halo, a paragraph whose hash is unchanged can legitimately change verdict when a distant paragraph introduces a contradiction with it, or when a new signposting move elsewhere shifts its role in the argument. The cache key `(paragraph_hash, check_id, model_used)` misses this. A soundness-preserving extension is `(paragraph_hash, check_id, model_used, round_fingerprint)` where `round_fingerprint` is a hash of the set of changed paragraphs at the round's inception — inheriting only when the diff-set matches. Defer P-14 until this is resolved.

**Response — ACCEPT, WITH REFINED PATCH. DEFER FROM v0.7.5 RC.**

**Response refinement.** The reviewer's exact-match `round_fingerprint` is correct on soundness but very restrictive in practice: the cache hits only when the current round's diff-set is byte-identical to the prior round's diff-set, which is rare across iterations. A softer variant that preserves soundness under the cross-paragraph-contamination concern is a **diff-scope subset key**: the cache hits when the current round's diff-set is a *subset* of the prior round's diff-set for cross-paragraph checks (contradiction, signposting), and matches by `(paragraph_hash, check_id, model_used)` alone for paragraph-local checks (sentence-level, first-use). This exploits the observation that paragraph-local checks have paragraph-scoped verdicts; only cross-paragraph checks require the round-scoped context.

**Action A-CK-1.** Extend P-14's cache key per-check-class: paragraph-local checks use `(paragraph_hash, check_id, model_used)`; cross-paragraph checks use `(paragraph_hash, check_id, model_used, diff_scope_fingerprint)` where `diff_scope_fingerprint` is the SHA-256 of the sorted changed-paragraph-id set. The check-class tag comes from the same `halo_scope` field added in Action A-HL-2.

**Action A-CK-2.** Defer P-14 from v0.7.5 RC. The deferral is explicit in the revised shipping scope at §4. P-14's soundness patch is non-trivial, its implementation complexity is the highest in the package, and its absence does not block the wall-clock gain from P-10 or the convergence tightening from P-12. P-14 is targeted at v0.7.6 with the threshold retuning milestone.

### 2.7. P-15 — routing determinism (reviewer §3, P-15)

**Reviewer's claim.** The F1/F4 routing determinism question is manageable but requires an explicit rule: "a finding whose primary evidence is sentence-level is filed to F1; a finding that incidentally observes a pattern belonging to a demoted check class is filed to F4-advisory." Without the rule, two runs on the same diff can route the same observation differently, surfacing at Phase 2g as spurious recurrence.

**Response — ACCEPT.**

**Action A-RT-1.** Add to P-15 a worked routing example in `agents/evaluator.md §3.2`: a finding's primary evidence is the check class under which the Evaluator first detected the issue (typically the check that fired its pre-filter). A finding whose primary evidence is `sentence-level-pass` is filed to F1 even if it also exhibits a demoted-class pattern. A finding whose primary evidence is a demoted-class pattern that the Evaluator noticed incidentally while running a different check is filed to F4-advisory. The Evaluator declares the routing decision at filing time with a `routing_rationale: "primary_evidence=<check_id>"` field in the finding frontmatter.

**Action A-RT-2.** Extend the Reflector's Phase 2f audit with `R-Refl-RT-1 routing_ambiguity` (severity MINOR): a finding whose `routing_rationale` field is missing, or whose primary-evidence check_id does not match the filing family (F1 for primary, F4-advisory for incidental), trips the audit.

### 2.8. P-16 — threshold derivation (reviewer §3, P-16)

**Reviewer's claim.** The 30% threshold for ceiling-lock offer is an unmotivated numeric parameter and should be derived from v0.7.4.1 retrospective data or marked provisional.

**Response — ACCEPT.** The critique mirrors P-12's threshold-derivation issue and merits the same treatment.

**Action A-TH-3.** Include P-16's 30% threshold in the same retrospective analysis proposed under Action A-TH-1. Derive the threshold from INF3006Y's iter-7 round (the round at which ceiling-lock would have been the rational move and was not offered) and any comparable rounds in INF3001H. If the derivation is under-powered, mark `30%` as `v0.7.5-provisional` with the same v0.7.6 retuning milestone as P-12.

**Action A-GK-3.** Add to the v0.7.5 proposal a new section **§2.1.1 — Ph4-deep classification falsification** (positioned as a refinement to P-9, adjacent to P-9a). ### 3.4. Advisor-escalation coupling (reviewer §4.4)

**Response — ACCEPT WITH SCOPING.**

**Response refinement.** The reviewer proposes an optional advisor-consultation hop on the Planner-suggested Ph3-deep invocation at the three-round-stall threshold.
