# Eubanks (2018): Narrative-Policy Style Guidelines Extracted from *Automating Inequality*

**Source:** Eubanks, V. (2018). *Automating inequality: How high-tech tools profile, police, and punish the poor*. St. Martin's Press.  

**Scope note:** This file distills style and argument-construction patterns from sampled sections of the manuscript (Introduction, chapter-body analysis, value framing, and methods notes). It is intended as an optional overlay for policy-critical writing, not a universal replacement for Baird/Sexton/Bacon controls.

---

## 1. Core voice pattern

- The prose frequently opens with a concrete, high-stakes lived incident before widening into system-level analysis.
- The writing combines humane narrative tone with explicit mechanism tracing (institutional process, technical rule, administrative consequence).
- Normative language is explicit, but it is paired with operational detail rather than left as abstract assertion.

---

## 2. Structural moves to imitate

### 2.1 Scene-first opening, then analytic expansion

- Start with a concrete scene involving identifiable actors and stakes.
- Transition to the broader system claim immediately after the scene to avoid anecdote-only framing.
- Keep the bridge explicit: personal episode -> policy/technical process -> broader pattern.

### 2.2 Mechanism-before-moralization

- For each evaluative claim, provide at least one traceable mechanism:
  - decision rule,
  - process design,
  - performance metric,
  - legal/procedural constraint.
- Avoid critique that is only tonal or rhetorical.

### 2.3 Case-to-system recursion

- Alternate between named cases and structural interpretation.
- Use case material to test, refine, or constrain general claims; do not treat cases as decorative illustrations.

---

## 3. Tone-of-voice guidance

- Empathetic but controlled: the prose can carry urgency while preserving analytical discipline.
- Direct and accessible: avoid unnecessary jargon when plain language can carry the claim.
- Normatively explicit: define value terms when used (for example, liberty, equity, inclusion) and specify the interpretation being applied.
- Non-sensational: avoid hyperbole when evidence can carry force.

---

## 4. Evidence discipline

- Distinguish observed facts, source-attributed claims, and interpretive inferences.
- Pair narrative testimony with documentary/quantitative/process evidence where available.
- Preserve traceability:
  - who said what,
  - what record supports the claim,
  - where uncertainty remains.
- Methods transparency is part of writing quality in this mode (interview handling, verification approach, limits).

---

## 5. Optional checklist (policy-critical mode)

Use this only when the manuscript is policy-critical, inequality-focused, or public-service accountability focused.

1. **Human anchor present:** major section opens with or quickly grounds in a concrete human-impact case.
2. **Mechanism named:** each major critique is tied to an operational mechanism.
3. **Normative axis defined:** value terms are defined in-context, not used as slogans.
4. **Evidence pair:** narrative claim is paired with institutional/process/documentary evidence.
5. **Burden and rights trace:** administrative burden, due process, surveillance, and access effects are explicit where relevant.
6. **No ornamental anecdote:** narrative material advances analysis.
7. **Methods transparency:** data collection/verification limitations are acknowledged.

## 7. Use boundaries

- Good fit: policy analysis, critical systems evaluation, public administration, social-impact sections, implications/discussion sections, response letters that must foreground lived impact.
- Caution: theory-heavy sections requiring impersonal register, methods sections where narrative expansion could dilute precision, and manuscripts whose venue norms disfavor first-person scene anchoring.
