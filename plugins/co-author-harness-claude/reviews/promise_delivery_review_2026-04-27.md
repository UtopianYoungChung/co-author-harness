# Promise–Delivery Review of `co-author-harness-claude` v0.10.2

> A PhD-tier audit conducted under the worry: *"the plugin does not deliver what it promised."*

## 1. Problem statement

The maintainer's worry is not a bug report; it is an **epistemic claim about contract fulfilment**. A plugin advertised as a "PhD-level academic-writing harness" with thirty-two skills, a four-rung lifecycle ladder, four agents, and a snowball-driven reference scaffold makes a thick promise — both at the level of *artefacts shipped* and at the level of *behaviours invoked*. The worry deserves to be tested against both surfaces. This review therefore treats the plugin as a **contract** and asks whether the contract is honoured at three levels: (i) the *manifest level* — what the plugin advertises about itself; (ii) the *substrate level* — what the filesystem actually ships; and (iii) the *operational level* — what the substrate enables a user to do. The first two are mechanically falsifiable. The third is where most plugins of this complexity fail without their maintainers noticing, because operational delivery is mediated by user discipline rather than by the substrate alone.

The substantive question is not whether the package *runs* — its four maintainer scripts pass green — but whether the gap between advertised promise and operational delivery is closing or widening across the v0.7.x → v0.10.x release arc. A plugin can pass every structural invariant it ships with and still drift from its public face.

## 2. Theoretical framework

I read the plugin through three lenses. **Requirements Engineering (RE)** distinguishes between functional promises (what the plugin does) and non-functional promises (how well it does it: discoverability, traceability, reproducibility, cognitive economy). The Volere template's atomicity-and-traceability discipline is the relevant standard: every advertised feature should be locatable, testable, and traceable to a contract. **Goal-Oriented RE (GORE)**, in the i\* and KAOS idiom of Yu and van Lamsweerde, frames the plugin as a network of stakeholder goals decomposed into tasks and softgoals. The plugin itself recognises this — `references/PHASE_PROTOCOL.md §2.1` literally describes a "GORE layer — four-phase goal decomposition." **Agent-Oriented RE (AORE)** in Yu's *Strategic Dependency* framing then asks who depends on whom for which goal, task, or resource — and where the dependencies are unsupported. The plugin's four named agents (Planner, Evaluator, Generator, Reflector) plus the human Researcher and the external MCP layer (Zotero, scholarly-search, advisor) form a six-actor SD network, and the promise–delivery question becomes: *which dependencies are honoured by shipped substrate, and which are honoured only by user discipline?*

Two HCI commitments inform the evaluative grain. First, *affordance theory* (Gibson via Norman) holds that a system's promise is communicated as much by its surface as by its capability — a 32-skill catalog signals deep capability but imposes a cognitive-load floor that can suppress invocation, even of skills that exist. Second, the **distinction between substitution and augmentation** (the plugin's own `REFLEXIVITY_CHECK.md` invokes this) frames the deepest delivery question: does the plugin *do* PhD-level review, or does it *help a discipline-bearing user perform* PhD-level review? These are different products with different promise budgets.

## 3. Methodology

I conducted the audit in five stages and grounded every claim in a filesystem read or a script execution rather than memory. **Stage 1 — Promise inventory.** I read `.claude-plugin/plugin.json`, `.claude-plugin/marketplace.json`, root `CLAUDE.md`, `README.md`, `CHANGELOG.md`, `references/CLAUDE.md`, the recent `RELEASE_NOTES_v0.10.0/.1/.2.md`, and the calibration ledger `.plugin-efficiency.json`, extracting explicit and implicit promises into a structured list. **Stage 2 — Substrate inventory.** I globbed `skills/`, `agents/`, `commands/`, `scripts/`, `references/`, `docs/agent-instructions/`, `legacy/`, and `releases/`, recording what is present and absent. **Stage 3 — Maintainer-script ground-truth.** I executed all four shipped invariant checks (`skill-check.py`, `version-check.py`, `catalog-check.py`, `path-hygiene-check.py`) and recorded their verdicts. **Stage 4 — Promise→delivery cross-walk** with severity classes: *Honoured*, *Honoured-with-caveat*, *Drifted*, *Undelivered*, *Conditional* (delivery requires opt-in or external resource). **Stage 5 — i\* SD synthesis.** I modelled the plugin's intentional structure as a Strategic Dependency graph and identified broken or unsupported dependencies — the locations where the maintainer's worry is most likely to be objectively grounded.

The methodology has one explicit limitation. I did not run the plugin end-to-end against a live manuscript; that would require a Cowork session with classification, phase_state ledger, and at least one section's prose. The audit therefore speaks to *contractual* delivery (what the substrate promises and supplies) and not to *empirical* delivery (whether a real review round produces PhD-tier output). This is a known boundary; §6 names the test fixtures that would close it.

## 4. Analysis

### 4.1 The structural invariants pass — the plugin honours its own internal contracts

Every maintainer script ships green. `skill-check.py` discovers thirty-two skills with zero blockers and zero warnings. `version-check.py` reports manifest, README, CHANGELOG, and marketplace.json all anchored at v0.10.2 with no skew. `catalog-check.py` reports thirty-two skills, sixteen commands, and the README's headline count agreeing — the v0.9.0 catalog-parity rule (every `commands/<name>.md` description is a prefix of the catalog Purpose) is satisfied. `path-hygiene-check.py` returns zero blockers. The plugin's own discipline of self-validation is therefore intact at the file-count and version-skew level. This is not nothing: the v0.10.0 RC carried two `marketplace.json` slips that the v0.10.1 hot-fixes closed at the validator level (a version skew between manifest and marketplace, and a `"."` source-format slip rejected by the marketplace loader). The validators that closed those slips are the same ones running now — and they are quiet. The maintainer should **not** infer from the worry that file-level promises are being broken; they are not.

### 4.2 Snowball reference scaffolding is delivered with full traceability — a successful promise

The most ambitious recent promise is the v0.10.0 snowball pipeline (SK-33 `seed-snowball-discovery`, SK-34 `claim-coverage-audit`, SK-35 `extend-snowball-incremental`, SK-36 `inherit-snowball-from-wiki`). All four skills exist on disk under `skills/`, are registered in `references/SKILL_REGISTRY.md` with explicit dependency declarations, are routed in `skills/plugin-commands/SKILL.md`, are wired into `skills/run-phase-1/SKILL.md` Step 4.5 and `skills/run-phase-2/SKILL.md` Step 0.5 for auto-dispatch, and have command shims under `commands/`. The architectural discipline is visible at a level few open-source plugins achieve: `.plugin-efficiency.json` carries five separate stage-rebase blocks (S1, S1.5, S2, S3, S4, S4.5) recording the projected cost-per-invocation drift across the rollout, with delta-attribution narratives that distinguish feature cost from bloat. The five-stage convergence on §6.0 of the snowball architecture document — three fix cycles converging on a coupling-checklist amendment, advisor consultation decisive at the round-2 inflection — is the strongest evidence in the repo that the maintainer is operating with PhD-tier methodological self-awareness about *changes to the plugin itself*. This promise is not the source of the worry.

### 4.3 The most severe drift is in the plugin's own governance trailers — three documents disagree about what version this even is

**Root `CLAUDE.md`, line 5:** *"the canonical home of the Research and Academic Paper Writing Package (harness substrate at __v0.8.7__)"*. **Root `CLAUDE.md`, line 9:** *"this repo root (`co-author-harness/`, __v0.8.7__+; formerly `research-writing-harness/`)"*. **`references/CLAUDE.md`, line 144:** *"Last updated: 2026-04-21 (__v0.7.4__ — Lifecycle-Phase Ladder and economic-efficiency package)."* **`.claude-plugin/plugin.json`:** *"version": "0.10.2"*. The harness's own *invocation rules* — the files Claude is meant to read first under the trigger table — therefore lag the manifest by three minor versions (root) and eight minor versions (package). The drift is not malicious; it reflects the substantial reality that since v0.7.4 the maintainer has shipped roughly twenty stage-close commits (v0.7.4 → v0.8.0 → v0.8.3 → v0.8.4 → v0.8.5 → v0.8.6 → v0.8.7 → v0.9.0 → v0.10.0 → v0.10.1 → v0.10.2), and the governance trailers were not in the rebase set. But for a user opening the plugin today, the *first authoritative document Claude reads* declares a substrate version that has not been current for at least nine days and several feature releases. **This is the single highest-severity drift in the audit.** It is also — diagnostically — the location most likely to provoke the maintainer's worry: it is the public-facing surface that *says what the plugin is*, and it says something stale.

### 4.4 Two README promises do not deliver against the filesystem

The README's *Repository layout* table includes `legacy/` ("Retired in-tree material — frozen reference, not the editing surface") and `releases/` ("Plugin `.zip` builds from `scripts/build-release-zip.sh` (when committed)"). A glob of `B:\Agents\co-author-harness\legacy\**` returns zero files. The directory does not exist. The README softens the `releases/` claim with the parenthetical "when committed," and the directory does exist — but it contains only `co-author-harness-claude-v0.8.4.zip` through `co-author-harness-claude-v0.8.7.zip`. There is no `v0.10.2.zip`. Neither claim is materially false (the README hedges `releases/`, and `legacy/` may have been a planned slot), but a sceptical reader who follows the layout table will find both promises unfulfilled. **Severity: low for `releases/` (hedge present, build script available); medium for `legacy/`** (no hedge, directory absent). The fix is either to delete the `legacy/` row or to create the directory with a `README.md` explaining what is retired in tree (likely nothing — the consolidation footnote in `harness-history.md` already names the retired `paper-harness/` and `research-writing-harness/` ancestors).

### 4.5 The keywords `i-star`, `GORE`, `AORE` advertise capability that is gated behind a default-off opt-in

The manifest's keyword array names `i-star`, `GORE`, and `AORE` in the same list as `research`, `academic-writing`, and `peer-review`. A reader will assume parity. But `references/PHASE_PROTOCOL.md §2.1` and `agents/planner.md §What you read` make plain that the i\*/GORE/AORE machinery — Strategic Dependency and Strategic Rationale model authoring, the `§3.1.1` structural-completeness validator, the `models/sd_model.md` and `models/sr_model.md` deliverables — fires **only when `sd_sr_required: true` in `reviews/classification.md`**. The default is `false`. The opt-in is real, the substrate is real, but the keyword list does not signal the gating. A user who installs the plugin expecting i\* model authoring out of the box will find none of the SD/SR machinery activates without a classification edit they have not been told about. **Severity: medium.** This is a discoverability gap, not a delivery gap — the capability ships, but the manifest's promise is louder than the operational on-ramp. The README's Quick Start does not name `sd_sr_required` either. The fix is either a one-line README addition naming the opt-in, or a keyword-list trim, or a default-on flip (with the existing absent-means-false migration semantics preserved for back-compat).

### 4.6 The manifest description is dense; the plugin requires a lookup before it can be used

The 540-character `description` field in `plugin.json` reads, in part: *"PhD academic-writing: four agents, Ph1-Ph4 ladder, MCR, phase_state, Check 8/§9d/§9e prefilters, provenance, snowball reference scaffolding (SK-33/34/35/36)."* Every term in that string is decodable from the substrate; none is wrong. But the description is unreadable to anyone outside the maintainer's head — it presupposes knowledge of the four-agent loop, the lifecycle-phase ladder, the Manuscript Convergence Report, the §9d / §9e DETERMINISTIC_CHECKS sections, and the SK-33–36 numbering scheme. The README mitigates this by leading with a one-sentence value proposition (*"PhD-level academic writing: four specialized agents, a climb-only phase ladder, and a rule stack that treats grounding as non-negotiable"*) — but the plugin marketplace (and Cowork plugin browser) shows the manifest description, not the README. **Severity: low-to-medium.** The plugin will not surface well in any UI surface that displays only the manifest description. A two-sentence rewrite — one human-readable, one technical — would pay for itself in install conversion.

### 4.7 The Strategic Dependency model exposes one structural fragility — the human Researcher carries unsupported dependencies

I model the plugin as a six-actor SD network: **Researcher** (human user), **Planner** (dispatcher agent), **Evaluator** (reviewer agent), **Generator** (writer agent), **Reflector** (lessons-and-drift agent), and **External Verifier Layer** (Zotero MCP, scholarly-search MCP, advisor MCP). The plugin substrate itself is a *resource* that all six actors depend on, not an actor.

```
i* Strategic Dependency (SD) — co-author-harness-claude v0.10.2
================================================================

Researcher ──D[goal: PhD-level reviewed manuscript]──> Planner
Researcher ──D[goal: phase-correct dispatch]─────────> Planner
Planner    ──D[task: classify-manuscript first]─────> Researcher    [UNSUPPORTED: depends on user discipline]
Planner    ──D[resource: reviews/classification.md]─> Researcher    [UNSUPPORTED: substrate cannot author]
Planner    ──D[task: independent review]──────────────> Evaluator
Planner    ──D[task: prose authoring & fix-apply]───> Generator
Planner    ──D[task: lessons + drift surfacing]─────> Reflector
Evaluator  ──D[resource: REFERENCES.md saturated]───> Generator      [via SK-33 if Ph1; else Researcher]
Evaluator  ──D[resource: external verifier hit]────> External Verifier Layer  [CONDITIONAL: Class 1 verifier required]
Reflector  ──D[softgoal: drift contained]──────────> All agents     [via DRIFT_CHECK.md]
Researcher ──D[softgoal: low cognitive load on entry]> Plugin Substrate  [PARTIALLY UNSUPPORTED: 32-skill floor]
Researcher ──D[task: i*/GORE/AORE modelling]───────> Planner         [CONDITIONAL: sd_sr_required: true opt-in]
Researcher ──D[resource: governance trailer in sync]> Plugin Substrate  [DRIFTED: root CLAUDE.md@v0.8.7, references/CLAUDE.md@v0.7.4, manifest@v0.10.2]
```

Three dependencies stand out as fragile. **First**, the Researcher's softgoal of *low cognitive load on entry* is partially unsupported by the substrate's 32-skill floor: the plugin-commands SKILL is the orientation skill, but a new user must already know to invoke `/plugin-commands` (or read README) to discover it. The QUICKSTART is a good mitigation but is buried in `references/`. **Second**, the Planner's dependency on the Researcher to *invoke `/classify-manuscript` first* is structurally unsupported: nothing in the substrate prevents a user from calling `/run-phase-3` on a project with no `reviews/classification.md`. The plugin-commands SKILL has a NEVER warning ("NEVER skip `/classify-manuscript`"), but warnings are not enforcement. **Third — and this is the high-severity one** — the Researcher's dependency on the Plugin Substrate for a *governance trailer in sync* is materially broken (§4.3). The Researcher reading the root `CLAUDE.md` is told the substrate is at v0.8.7; the Researcher reading the `references/CLAUDE.md` trailer is told v0.7.4. The substrate is at v0.10.2. This is the SD broken edge that most likely powers the maintainer's worry, because it is the *first* dependency a fresh session resolves.

### 4.8 Three softer concerns worth naming

**(i) The plugin has no end-to-end test fixture.** The smoketest fixtures under `scripts/fixtures/` exercise migration scripts (`phase_state_smoketest`, `paragraph_hash_map_smoketest`, `convergence_journal_smoketest`, `artefact_frontmatter_smoketest`) and the snowball graph substrate (`snowball_graph_substrate_smoketest`) — these are unit-level. There is no fixture that runs `/classify-manuscript` → `/run-phase-1` → `/run-phase-2` → `/run-phase-3` on a sample manuscript and asserts that the four agents produce the expected artefact set. This is the test that would close the *operational* delivery gap — the one that asks not "does the substrate exist?" but "does the substrate *work*?" Building it is not trivial because Cowork sessions are interactive, but a stub manuscript with a sample classification and a recorded transcript could establish baseline behaviour. **Severity: medium.** The maintainer's worry is partially a worry about the absence of this fixture — without it, "delivery" is a contract claim, not a measured outcome.

**(ii) The plugin's value is concentrated in 5–7 daily-use skills; the other 25 are research depth.** This is a feature, not a bug, but it interacts with the cognitive-load floor: a user evaluating the plugin against a competitor may count to 32 and feel the surface is overgrown. A *headline* vs. *depth* distinction in the README (rather than a flat "Skills (32)") would help. The plugin-commands SKILL routing table already has this structure implicitly; lifting it into the README is a low-cost edit.

**(iii) The keyword `EXTERNAL_VERIFIERS` in the manifest is an aspirational dependency.** The plugin's `references/EXTERNAL_VERIFIERS.md §1.5 Rule 7a` requires Class 1 verifier capability for submission-bound rounds. The maintainer's deployment has Zotero MCP and the scholarly-search MCP wired (per `references/CLAUDE.md` 2026-04-13 addendum). A new user has neither. The plugin therefore promises a rule it cannot enforce on a fresh install, and its grounding discipline degrades silently (per `Rule 7a`, the round becomes unconditionally blocked at submission-bound depth, but this surfaces only when a user attempts to ship). **Severity: low** — the plugin is honest about the dependency, and the failure mode is fail-closed (block submission rather than ship a hallucination) — but the install path does not name the prerequisite. A README "Prerequisites" subsection naming Zotero MCP and the scholarly-search MCP, with links, would close the affordance gap.

## 5. Synthesis

The maintainer's worry — *"the plugin does not deliver what it promised"* — is **partly correct, but located differently than instinct suggests**. The plugin's *file-level* and *invariant-level* promises are honoured; thirty-two skills ship, four agents ship, the four-rung ladder ships, the snowball pipeline ships, the calibration discipline is unusually rigorous, and every maintainer script returns green. The worry is not grounded at the surface where it would normally hide (broken catalogs, missing scripts, manifest skew).

The worry is grounded *one level up* — at the **governance trailer**, where root `CLAUDE.md` declares v0.8.7 and `references/CLAUDE.md` declares v0.7.4 against a v0.10.2 manifest. This is the *first* artefact a fresh Claude session reads, and it tells a story about the substrate that has not been true for over a week and several feature releases. A plugin's most expensive drift is rarely in the code; it is in the document the user reads to learn what the code is. That is exactly where the drift here sits.

Two further worries are real but less load-bearing. The keyword-vs-opt-in mismatch on `i-star`/`GORE`/`AORE` is a real discoverability gap, but it is a *promise overshoot* (saying more than the default delivers) rather than a *promise underdelivery* (failing to ship a stated capability). The README's `legacy/` row is a genuine empty promise but trivially fixed. The absence of an end-to-end test fixture is the most consequential gap if the maintainer wants to escape a worry like this in future — an audit grounded in *contracts* will always be partially in the dark; an audit grounded in *measurements* removes that uncertainty.

## 6. Concrete contributions

I name nine fixes with severity tags and order-of-effort estimates. The first three retire the high-severity governance drift; the next three retire the medium-severity discoverability and substrate gaps; the last three are the long-cycle hardening that would make a future audit unnecessary.

1. **HIGH — bump root `CLAUDE.md` version trailer from v0.8.7 to v0.10.2** (lines 5 and 9). One commit; ~15 min. Closes §4.3's worst SD edge.
2. **HIGH — bump `references/CLAUDE.md` trailer from `2026-04-21 (v0.7.4 …)` to `2026-04-27 (v0.10.2 …)`** with a one-paragraph addendum naming the v0.7.4 → v0.10.2 deltas (lifecycle-phase ladder schema growth 15→18 fields, snowball SK-33–36, Sub-check H, §9d/§9e prefilters). One commit; ~30 min. Closes §4.3's second SD edge.
3. **HIGH — extend `version-check.py` to read the root `CLAUDE.md` and `references/CLAUDE.md` trailers** and fail BLOCKER on a version-skew with the manifest. The existing `extract_marketplace_self_referencing_versions` pattern is the template. One commit; ~1 hr. Prevents recurrence of the §4.3 drift.
4. **MEDIUM — add a README "Prerequisites" subsection naming Zotero MCP and the scholarly-search MCP** as Class 1 verifier dependencies; link to `references/EXTERNAL_VERIFIERS.md §1.5 Rule 7a`. One commit; ~20 min. Closes §4.8(iii)'s install-path gap.
5. **MEDIUM — name the `sd_sr_required: true` opt-in in the README and the manifest description** (or trim `i-star`/`GORE`/`AORE` from the keyword list). One commit; ~15 min. Closes §4.5's discoverability gap.
6. **MEDIUM — delete the `legacy/` row from the README repository-layout table** (or create the directory with a one-line `README.md`). One commit; ~5 min. Closes §4.4's empty-promise.
7. **MEDIUM — rewrite the manifest `description` for human readability**. Two-sentence target: a value sentence (audience: marketplace browser) followed by a technical sentence (audience: maintainer). One commit; ~30 min. Closes §4.6.
8. **LOW — restructure the README's "Skills (32)" line into a headline-vs-depth split** (e.g., "8 daily-use skills; 24 research-depth overlays"). The plugin-commands routing table already has this structure. One commit; ~30 min. Closes §4.8(ii).
9. **LOW (long-cycle) — author an end-to-end smoketest fixture** that runs the full Ph1→Ph2→Ph3 ladder on a 200-word stub manuscript and asserts the artefact set. The fixture goes under `scripts/fixtures/end_to_end_ladder_smoketest/`. Multi-session effort; ~4–8 hours. Closes §4.8(i) and removes the structural reason a future audit must speak in contract terms rather than measurement terms.

The first three items together would resolve the maintainer's stated worry. The next three would close the operational on-ramp. The last three are the strategic capacity-building that makes a "does the plugin deliver?" question answerable in seconds rather than in three pages of cross-walk.

## 7. Reflexive note

I conducted this audit against a six-month-young memory of this plugin's own conventions (per the memory file `co_author_harness_v0_10_0`) and four executions of the maintainer scripts. I did **not** consult `references/DRIFT_LOG.md` or the `DRIFT_CHECK_REPORT_*.md` files; if those reports already name §4.3, this review is duplicative on that finding and the maintainer should treat their audit-of-record as authoritative. I also did not run the plugin against a manuscript; §6 item 9 names the fixture that would let the next audit do so. The most defensible reading of this review is therefore: *the substrate-level promises are honoured at v0.10.2; the manifest-level promises overshoot the substrate by a discoverable margin in two places; and the governance-trailer drift is the load-bearing source of the maintainer's worry.* The fix is a small commit set away.
